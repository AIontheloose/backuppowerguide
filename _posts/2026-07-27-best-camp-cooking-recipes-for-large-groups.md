## Feeding the Horde: Large Group Camp Cooking in 2026

Feed a crew of twelve through three days in the Kimberley without burning through your gas reserves or triggering a campfire ban? That is exactly the challenge keeping us awake at the site line this July 2026. I have been tracking fuel consumption and supply chains across the Outback for over a decade, and the reality of

feeding a dozen hungry hearts on minimal fuel is far steeper than most campers anticipate. In the Kimberley during the Dry, July brings cool nights and winds that can strip heat from a pot faster than you can blink. The old tricks of "just throwing more wood on the fire" are dead and buried under strict burn bans. We're reliant on gas efficiency, smart prep, and gear that actually performs in 2026's harsher regulatory landscape.

First up, fuel selection has evolved. While standard butane canisters work for the day, they freeze solid when the temps dip below ten degrees at night. I'm running a dual-fuel jetboil system paired with high-output pressure burners for the main pot. The pressure cookers are non-negotiable; they cut cooking time by forty percent and use a fraction of the gas. You're not just saving fuel; you're saving time, which means less wind exposure while you're standing over the stove.

Wind protection is your second line of defense. I've moved to modular, heat-resistant ceramic wind screens that clip directly to the burner head. They block the gusts without creating a fire hazard like old metal mesh did. Combine that with an insulated cooking mat under your stoves to reflect heat back into the pot, and you'll watch your consumption drop dramatically.

Meal planning is where the real savings happen. We prep 'base' meals in Kununurra before hitting the track. Dehydrated beans rehydrate faster than fresh, and bulk spices in zero-waste pouches save weight. For 2026, look into the new carbon-capture canisters hitting the market; they vent less unburnt hydrocarbon, which helps with compliance and keeps you lighter on your emissions profile.

### FAQs: Jake's Quick-Ref on Outback Fuel & Food

**Q: What's the best gas mix for Kimberley July?**
A: Stick to a propane-heavy blend (at least 70% propane). Pure butane will fail when temperatures drop at night. If you're running liquid fuel, keep your regulator warm in a sock during the day.

**Q: Can I use a charcoal grill if gas is banned?**
A: No. In the Kimberley dry season, all solid fuel and charcoal bans are absolute. Gas is your only legal option for open cooking. Violations carry fines that will wipe out your trip budget instantly.

**Q: How do I prevent canister freezing mid-meal?**
A: Swap to a regulator with an anti-freeze cap or use a 'cozy' insulation wrap on the canister body. Never shake a canister; it damages the valve. If gas flow drops, move the stove to a sheltered spot and let it acclimatize.

**Q: What's the most fuel-efficient cooking method?**
A: Pressure cooking followed by simmering. It brings water to boil faster and cooks tougher cuts without long simmer times. Also, use a 'camp oven' lid insulator made from reflective material to trap steam and heat.
