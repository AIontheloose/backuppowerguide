## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Does "Unlimited" mean uncapped speeds on prepaid?**
A: No. In 2026, "unlimited" refers to data volume, not speed. Most prepaid plans include a high-speed allowance (e.g., 20–40 GB of capped high-speed data), after which your speed is throttled to a lower tier (often 2 Mbps or 150 Mbps depending on the network) for the remainder of the billing cycle.

**Q: Will deprioritization affect my voice calls and texts?**
A: Generally, no. Voice calls and SMS are routed via VoLTE and VoNR protocols which maintain high priority across all networks. Deprioritization primarily impacts data sessions. You may still experience minor latency spikes during extreme network congestion, but call quality remains stable on 5G/LTE.

**Q: How do I know if my phone supports Australian 5G bands?**
A: Ensure your device supports the n78 (3.5 GHz) and n28 (700 MHz) bands for Telstra/Optus, and n78/n79 for Vodafone. Most smartphones sold in Australia since 2023 include these bands. Check your manufacturer's specs or look for "Multi-Gigabit 5G" certification to verify compatibility.

**Q: Are MVNOs like dodo reliable for 5G?**
A: Yes. MVNOs ride on the same infrastructure as major carriers. If you have signal on Telstra, Optus, or Vodafone, you will get that network's 5G speed profile on an MVNO. The trade-off is that MVNO users are often deprioritized even further than postpay subscribers during congestion, but for general use, performance is indistinguishable from the parent brand.

**Q: Can I upgrade my prepaid plan to get priority 5G?**
A: Usually, no. Priority data access is a feature reserved for postpay contracts and enterprise accounts. Prepaid plans, regardless of price tier, are subject to fair use policies and deprioritization. If priority is critical, you must switch to a postpay SIM.

## Conclusion

Navigating the prepaid market in 2026 demands a pragmatic approach to connectivity. While 5G is universally available across major networks, the experience for prepaid users is heavily mediated by speed caps and deprioritization policies. The "unlimited" label now primarily denotes data volume rather than performance priority. For most consumers, MVNOs offer the sweet spot, providing reliable 4G/5G access at competitive price points without the premium markup of postpay contracts. However, if you reside in a fringe coverage area or require consistent high-bandwidth reliability for professional use, Telstra's priority grid may justify the cost. Ultimately, your decision should hinge on local signal strength, realistic usage habits, and a clear understanding that fair use policies still shape your daily experience. Choose based on coverage and value, not just marketing promises.
