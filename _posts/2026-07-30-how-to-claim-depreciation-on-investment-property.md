## How to Claim Depreciation on an Investment Property in 2026

In 2026, the Australian tax framework continues to reward investors who understand how to systematically document and claim property depreciation. While headlines frequently cite headline figures such as a $25 000 annual reduction in taxable income, those numbers only materialise when investors track capital works and plant & equipment with precision. Consider a straightforward example: an investor purchases a ducted air‑conditioning system for $4 800. Under the diminishing‑value method, that single asset generates roughly $720 in first‑year depreciation. Across multiple units or commercial properties, these figures compound rapidly. This guide breaks down the mechanics of property depreciation under current ATO guidelines, outlines compliance requirements, and provides a data‑driven framework for maximising claims while managing risk.

---

### 📜 Understanding the Tax Framework

#### 1. Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 – The Legislative Backbone  
Depreciation claims fall under Division 40 of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997*. The legislation distinguishes between capital works (building structure) and plant & equipment (detachable assets). Recent compliance audits have tightened documentation standards, meaning investors must now retain purchase contracts, progress reports, and quantity surveyor schedules to substantiate claims during ATO reviews.

#### 2. Cap Hierarchy – Instant Deduction vs. Annual Limit  
The tax system applies two distinct caps, which operate independently:
- **Instant deduction cap:** Up to $10 000 per income year for eligible plant & equipment items acquired after 30 June 2023.
- **Annual depreciation cap:** A maximum of $25 000 in total depreciable assets can be claimed each financial year. Excess amounts are carried forward to subsequent years without time limits.

| Cap Type | Threshold | Application | Carry‑Forward Rule |
|----------|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Instant Deduction | $10 000/year | Eligible plant & equipment purchased during the year | No carry‑forward; unused cap expires |
| Annual Depreciation Limit | $25 000/year | Total of all depreciable assets claimed | Excess carried forward indefinitely |

#### 3. Immediate Write‑Off vs. Diminishing‑Value Method  
- **Instant deduction items:** Typically include low‑cost electronics, kitchen appliances, and certain security systems. These qualify for a 100 % claim in the financial year of acquisition, provided they meet the $10 000 cap.
- **Diminishing‑value method:** Applies to higher‑value plant & equipment such as HVAC systems, lifts, and fitted furniture. Depreciation is calculated annually at 15 % on the reducing balance of the asset’s cost base.

> **Definition Box:** *Instant Deduction = 100 % claim in the year purchased (e.g., appliances). Plant & Equipment = 15 % diminishing‑value method applied to the remaining book value each year.*

#### 4. Post‑30 June 2023 Property Rules  
Properties acquired after 30 June 2023 are subject to stricter depreciation eligibility. Investors can no longer claim a first‑year instant deduction on new construction or major renovations unless the assets meet specific plant & equipment thresholds. All depreciable items must be calculated over their effective life using the diminishing‑value method, which extends the claim timeline but improves long‑term cash flow stability.

#### 5. Capital Gains Tax (CGT) Interaction  
Depreciation reduces annual taxable income but directly impacts your CGT cost base upon disposal. The ATO requires all claimed depreciation to be added back to the property’s cost base. For example, if you have claimed $20 000 in depreciation over five years and sell the asset for a $150 000 capital gain, your assessable gain becomes $130 000. This adjustment lowers your eventual CGT liability, but it also means depreciation is not a “free” deduction; it is a timing mechanism that defers tax rather than eliminating it.

#### 6. Special Circumstances – SMSF & Superannuation Integration  
Investors utilising self‑managed super funds (SMSFs) must navigate separate compliance pathways. Depreciation claims within an SMSF still reduce the fund’s taxable income, but CGT concessions, in‑house asset rules, and borrowing restrictions apply differently. If your property is held through a trust structure or SMSF, align depreciation strategies with broader superannuation contribution caps and preservation age thresholds before filing returns.

### 🛠️ How to Build Your Depreciation Schedule

#### 1. Gather Primary Documentation  
Compile purchase invoices, builder progress claims, strata records, and renovation contracts. The ATO requires contemporaneous evidence; post‑hoc estimates are routinely disallowed during compliance reviews.

#### 2. Categorise Assets by Depreciation Type  
- **Capital works:** Structural components (walls, roofing, foundations). Claimed at 2.5 % straight‑line over 40 years from construction date.
- **Plant & equipment:** Detachable assets (air‑conditioning, carpets, appliances). Claimed using the diminishing‑value method at 15 % annually.

#### 3. Apply the Correct Depreciation Formula  
For plant & equipment, calculate annual claims using:

\[
\text{Annual Depreciation} = \text{Cost Base} \times 15\% \times \frac{\text{Days Held}}{365}
\]

Apply this formula to each asset category, pool similar items where ATO rules permit, and track the reducing balance annually. Never double‑count capital works alongside plant & equipment claims.

#### 4. Verify Caps & Carry‑Forward Balances  
Cross‑check total claims against the $25 000 annual limit. If your schedule exceeds this threshold, allocate the excess to the next financial year. Maintain a depreciation ledger to ensure continuity across multiple income years and prevent ATO disputes.

### ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: Can I claim depreciation on a property I bought before 30 June 2023 but still hold it in 2026?**  
*A1:* Yes, you can continue claiming depreciation on assets acquired prior to the 2023 policy shift. The instant deduction cap and diminishing‑value calculations remain applicable for those original purchases. However, any new assets added after 30 June 2023 must follow the updated plant & equipment depreciation pathways. Maintaining a continuous asset register ensures your claims remain compliant and fully substantiated during ATO assessments.

**Q2: Will claiming depreciation affect my superannuation contributions?**  
*A2:* Directly, no. Depreciation deductions reduce your taxable rental income but do not alter your personal or employer super guarantee obligations. Indirectly, a lower taxable profit may improve your net cash flow, potentially freeing up discretionary funds for voluntary concessional or non‑concessional super contributions. Always verify contribution caps and preservation age thresholds before reallocating rental surplus into superannuation strategies.

**Q3: What happens if I sell the property before 10 years?**  
*A3:* The accumulated depreciation you have claimed is added back to the property’s cost base for capital gains tax purposes. This adjustment reduces your assessable capital gain, which can significantly lower your CGT liability upon disposal. For example, $30 000 in historical depreciation will reduce your taxable gain by that exact amount, provided you retain accurate records. Early sellers should model this cost‑base uplift during exit planning to avoid unexpected tax surprises.

**Q4: Do I need a quantity surveyor for every property?**  
*A4:* Not necessarily. Investors with single dwellings and straightforward renovation histories can often achieve accurate claims through verified software packages. Quantity surveyors become essential when managing multi‑storey complexes, mixed‑use developments, or properties with extensive commercial plant & equipment. The audit cost is typically recovered within 18–24 months due to the identification of overlooked assets and compliance risk mitigation.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can I claim depreciation on a second-hand residential property?**  
A: Only for plant and equipment items you install after acquisition. The 2017 legislative changes capped deductions for existing assets at $25,000 unless you hold a pre-May 2017 quantity surveyor report or can substantiate that the assets were acquired before that date. Always verify your position against current ATO rulings and keep purchase agreements intact.

**Q: How often should I update my depreciation schedule?**  
A: Industry best practice dictates a fresh audit every five years, or immediately following substantial renovations, changes in tenancy mix, or disposal of fixtures. Your diminishing-value or prime-cost balances must be recalculated to reflect the property’s current condition and your ongoing tax year alignment.

**Q: What happens to unclaimed depreciation when I sell?**  
A: Unclaimed plant and equipment deductions do not convert into cash refunds, but they can be incorporated into your cost base for capital gains tax calculations. Conversely, any remaining construction-related capital works balance may trigger a recoupment calculation under Division 43 of the Income Tax Assessment Act upon disposal.

**Q: How does depreciation interact with negative gearing?**  
A: Depreciation is a non-cash deduction that amplifies your tax offset without impacting cash flow. When strategically layered with interest, strata, and maintenance claims, it can meaningfully reduce taxable income—though it should never replace rigorous cash flow modelling or long-term hold assumptions.

**Q: Is the $25,000 cap permanent?**  
A: It remains in effect for second-hand residential properties unless you qualify for grandfathering provisions or can prove pre-2017 acquisition of assets. Legislative reviews are periodic, but no wholesale reversal has been enacted to date. Maintain documentation and seek annual compliance checks.
