### The State of Shower Screens in Australia: 2026 Market Reality

Before we look at picks, let's talk numbers so you can budget with confidence. A common myth is that most shower screens are made locally these days. However, according to the *Australian Glass & Ceramics Association* 2025 market report, only approximately **27%** of all bathroom glass products are sourced from domestic manufacturers. Local production is actually concentrated mainly in Victoria and Western Australia, with Queensland and New South Wales accounting for less than 12% each.

The bulk of screens are still imported from Asia, but don't let that discourage you! The good news is that supply chains have stabilised, and competitive pricing remains strong. With the current exchange rate at **1 USD = 1.42 AUD**, imported screens have seen a slight price adjustment, making local stockists even more competitive for mid-range options. Plus, choosing durable materials now means fewer replacements down the track, which is the ultimate budget saver.

### Why Your Shower Screen Needs to Fight Back in 2026

Australian bathrooms face unique challenges that overseas products often ignore. From the salt-laden air of coastal regions in Byron Bay to the intense humidity that can turn a suburban bathroom into a steam room, your screen needs to work hard.

In 2026, we're seeing a massive shift toward climate-ready materials. Tempered glass with advanced anti-frost coatings is now standard for durability against condensation, and frames are engineered with superior anti-corrosion treatments to survive years of hot showers without turning into rust buckets.

**Safety First:** Regulatory standards under **AS/NZS 3500** have tightened up. All shower enclosures must meet strict requirements regarding glass safety and slip resistance. When evaluating screens, always verify that the material has a minimum coefficient of friction greater than 0.45 under wet conditions. Safety is non-negotiable in our homes, and reputable brands will gladly provide this data sheet.

### Mastering the Measurements: A Step-by-Step Sizing Guide

One of the biggest frustrations I hear from DIYers is buying a screen that doesn't fit because walls aren't perfectly plumb. Tiles can vary in thickness, and old buildings have personalities!

Here is my foolproof method for measuring your niche:
1.  **Measure Width:** Take three measurements (top, middle, bottom). Use the smallest measurement as your base width.
2.  **Deduct Gaps:** Subtract 5 mm to 10 mm from your width to allow for expansion gaps and sealant. This ensures the screen doesn't bind against the wall.
3.  **Measure Height:** Measure from the top of the shower tray or tile up to your desired height. Again, use the smallest measurement.
4.  **Check Square:** If you are installing a pivot door, measure diagonals from corner to corner. If they differ by more than 5 mm, your niche isn't square, and you'll need adjustable hardware.

> **Pro Tip:** Don't rely solely on a tape measure for this. Use a digital laser distance meter to get precise readings without the hassle of stretching across the shower. You can grab reliable measuring tools via [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com.au/s?k=digital+laser+distance+meter&tag=owlno-22) to make sure your numbers are spot on before you order.

**How often should I reseal the edges?**  
Silicone sealant typically lasts 3–5 years depending on humidity levels and cleaning products used. Inspect it annually for cracking, peeling, or mould around joints. Replace with a high-quality, mildew-resistant silicone formulated specifically for wet areas to prevent hidden water damage behind tiles and walls.

**Does hydrophobic glass actually repel water?**  
Yes. Hydrophobic coatings create a microscopic barrier that causes water to bead and roll off rather than sit and evaporate into hard-water stains. Most reputable manufacturers apply these during the tempering process, but reapplication every 2–3 years with a compatible spray can restore beading performance.

**Are there eco-friendly or sustainable options?**  
Frameless designs reduce overall material use, while recycled aluminium frames are gaining traction in Australian markets. Look for low-VOC sealants, water-based protective coatings, and manufacturers with Green Building Council certification. Many brands now offer end-of-life recycling programs for glass and metal components.
