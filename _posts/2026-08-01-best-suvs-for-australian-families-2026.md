*   **ANCAP Safety Rating:** All models listed carry a 5

star rating, but don’t stop at the badge. Look for active safety tech that actually works in Australian conditions: autonomous emergency braking (AEB) tuned for wildlife and heavy vehicles, blind-spot monitoring with cross-traffic alert, and lane-keep assist that doesn’t tug the wheel on corrugated shoulders. If you tow, verify braked vs unbraked limits and ensure electronic stability control includes trailer sway mitigation.

*   **Cooling & Climate Control:** Dual-zone climate with pollen/cabin air filters is essential for summer road trips. Look for rear-seat ventilation or dedicated AC vents if kids share the back row.
*   **Service Intervals & Warranty:** Prioritise brands offering 7-year/unlimited-kilometre warranties and capped-price servicing. Outback travel accelerates wear, so extended coverage and clear service schedules save thousands over ownership.

### FAQ: Family SUV Buying & Maintenance in Australia

**Q: What’s the best engine type for Australian family SUVs?**  
Turbo-diesel or 2.0L turbo-petrol delivers the torque needed for towing, hill climbs, and high-heat cruising. Hybrids excel in cities but add complexity for remote travel. Avoid naturally aspirated engines under 1.6L—they’ll overwork in dust and on inclines.

**Q: How do I protect my SUV from red dust and coastal salt?**  
Wash the underbody monthly, especially after coastal drives or outback trips. Apply a ceramic coating to paint and use rubber sealant on door/window edges. Replace cabin air filters every 6 months if you run with windows down regularly.

**Q: Are 7-seater SUVs worth the extra cost?**  
Only if you frequently carry more than four people or need flexible cargo folding. Third-row seats in most family SUVs are tight for adults over 10. For serious space, consider a dual-cab utility with a canopy or a dedicated people-mover.

**Q: What tyre pressure should I run on mixed Australian roads?**  
Stick to manufacturer recommendations (usually 32–35 PSI) for highways. Drop by 4–6 PSI for gravel or sand, but reinflate before returning to sealed roads. Always carry a digital gauge and portable compressor.

**Q: When should I choose a dual-cab utility over an SUV?**  
If you tow heavy loads, carry bulky gear regularly, or frequently drive on unsealed tracks, a 4x4 utility will outlast and outperform most family SUVs. Otherwise, stick with a high-riding SUV for comfort, safety tech, and daily drivability.
