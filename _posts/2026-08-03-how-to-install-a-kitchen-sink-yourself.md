### 🛑 Safety & Compliance First

Before we grab our wrenches, let's talk rules and protection. DIY is brilliant, but your health and compliance come first.

*   **PPE Essentials:** Always wear cut-resistant gloves when handling metal edges and safety glasses when cutting pipes or drilling. A dust mask is wise if you're removing old silicone or grout.
*   **Australian Plumbing Code (APC):** Ensure your new setup complies with the current Australian Plumbing Code. Most standard sink replacements are considered "non-major" work, but if you're altering drainage lines or connecting to mains, check with your local council.
*   **Sink Materials:** While this guide focuses on stainless steel for its durability and value, these steps apply to most drop-in sinks. If you're installing a heavy **cast-iron**, **composite**, or **fireclay** model, ensure your cabinet support can handle the extra weight to prevent sagging.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Can I safely install a kitchen sink and tap myself, or should I hire a plumber?**  
A: If you’re comfortable with basic hand tools and follow step-by-step instructions, DIY is completely achievable. Just remember to shut off the water supply, work slowly on connections, and test thoroughly before finalising.

**Q: What’s the best silicone for sealing around a new kitchen sink?**  
A: Use a 100% neutral-cure silicone specifically labelled for kitchen and bathroom applications. It won’t corrode stainless steel or stone surfaces, dries clear, and resists mould and temperature shifts better than standard acrylics.

**Q: Why does my tap drip after installation?**  
A: Most drips come from a misaligned washer, overtightened cartridge, or debris trapped in the valve seat. Disassemble carefully, inspect seals, clean internal passages, and reassemble without forcing components.

**Q: How do I ensure proper drain alignment with an existing waste pipe?**  
A: Use a flexible braided waste connector if your sink position differs slightly from the old one. Never force rigid pipes into place—misalignment creates stress fractures and slow drainage over time.

**Q: Are there water-saving upgrades worth considering?**  
A: Absolutely. Look for taps with a 4-star WaterMark rating or higher, and install an aerator if yours doesn’t include one. These small additions cut flow by up to 30% without compromising pressure or usability.
