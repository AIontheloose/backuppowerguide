### FAQ: Common Questions for Aussie Wrenchers (Continued)

**Q: Can I really use a basic OBD-II scanner on a 2026 hybrid or electric vehicle?**  
A: Not effectively. While a basic **OBD‑II scanner** will read standard emission codes, it lacks the high-voltage isolation and manufacturer-specific protocols required to safely access battery management systems, inverter temperatures, or regenerative braking modules. For hybrids and EVs, you need a dedicated **EV diagnostic tool** that complies with ISO 15118 and SAE J3068 standards, supports DoIP and UDS diagnostics, and includes proper safety interlocks for high-voltage systems.

**Q: Do I need special certification before touching a high-voltage EV battery?**  
A: Absolutely. In Australia, you’ll need at least an ECP (Electrical Competence) or equivalent low/high-voltage electrical safety ticket per AS/NZS 3000 and the relevant state regulations. Never bypass isolation procedures or assume a “discharged” pack is safe. Real wrenching means respecting the kill switches, PPE, and lockout/tagout protocols—no shortcuts.

**Q: Will these tools work on older EVs like early Leaf models or first-gen Teslas?**  
A: Yes, but with caveats. Proprietary systems like Tesla’s early CAN architecture require compatible level-2/3 diagnostics that can bypass dealer-only restrictions. Older Nissan Leafs often need specialized software to access the BMS directly. Always verify tool compatibility lists before buying—don’t assume “EV-ready” means “universal-ready.”

**Q: How often should I calibrate or update my diagnostic equipment?**  
A: Monthly, at minimum. EV firmware updates roll out constantly via OTA, and diagnostic protocols shift with every major pack redesign. Keep your tool’s subscription active, download the latest vendor-specific calibration files, and cross-reference VINs against manufacturer service bulletins. Stale software is worse than no software.
