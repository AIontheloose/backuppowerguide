### How Capital Gains Tax Actually Works on Property

CGT in Australia isn’t a standalone levy; it’s simply the taxable portion of your profit when you sell an asset that has increased in value. The Australian Taxation Office (ATO) treats property as a capital asset, meaning your gain is calculated by subtracting your cost base from the net sale proceeds. Your cost base includes the purchase price, stamp duty, legal fees, agent commissions, and eligible improvement costs. Crucially, the 2026 framework now explicitly allows borrowing interest incurred during the construction or substantial renovation phase to be added to the cost base, provided the works are completed within a reasonable timeframe. Land tax adjustments where applicable can also be factored into the long-term holding period calculation, though they do not directly reduce the taxable gain.

The most critical mechanism for individual investors remains the 50% CGT discount. If you hold the property for more than 12 months before selling, you’re entitled to exclude exactly half of the capital gain from your taxable income. This discount doesn’t apply to companies or superannuation funds, but it remains one of the most powerful wealth-building mechanisms available to individual Australians. I recommend running scenario models early in your investment timeline because the 12-month holding period is non-negotiable for this benefit, and missing it can effectively double your tax liability on the same asset appreciation.

### Worked Example: Translating Theory into Tax Liability

Understanding the mechanics requires a concrete walkthrough. Consider an investor who purchases a property in 2024 for $500,000. They pay $30,000 in stamp duty, $5,000 in legal fees, and $50,000 on eligible structural renovations completed within the holding period. Their total cost base is $585,000. In late 2026, they sell for $950,000, paying $19,000 in agent commissions. Net sale proceeds are $931,000. The gross capital gain sits at $346,000 ($931,000 - $585,000). Because the asset was held for over 12 months, the 50% discount applies, reducing the taxable gain to $173,000. This amount is added to their marginal income and taxed at their applicable bracket, demonstrating why accurate cost base tracking is non-negotiable for net portfolio returns.

### Primary Residence Exemption and Defining “Main Home”

Australia’s main home exemption is famously generous, but it’s also heavily misunderstood by first-time investors. If a property qualifies as your primary residence, you can claim full CGT relief, provided you meet the residency test. Generally, this means you’ve lived in the property for at least six months of your ownership period, or continuously if you owned it for less than five years without using another property as your main home. The ATO strictly differentiates between a primary residence and a holiday home; the latter never qualifies for full exemption regardless of occupancy frequency.

Complications arise quickly when you repurpose a property. Converting your home into a rental while moving out triggers partial CGT liability from the date of first use as an income-producing asset. Similarly, buying a new home before selling the old one can temporarily complicate your exemption status under ATO rules. The temporary absence rule allows you to continue treating a former main home as exempt for up to six years after moving out, provided you don’t claim any other property as your main home during that window. Understanding these boundaries prevents unexpected tax assessments when lifestyle or market conditions force a change in occupancy strategy.

### 2026 Legislative Adjustments and Market Realities

The regulatory landscape shifted in 2026 with two notable adjustments impacting property investors. First, the ATO introduced a CGT on rental-property depreciation adjustment that prevents double-dipping. Previously, investors could claim income tax deductions for depreciating assets while also inflating their cost base upon sale. The new rule requires you to subtract claimed or notional depreciation from your cost base, ensuring only genuine capital appreciation is taxed.

Second, a temporary first-time buyer CGT relief was implemented for designated suburban growth corridors. Eligibility requires purchasing your first property post-2024 within approved local government boundaries, holding it for at least three years, and using it as your primary residence during that period. The relief caps at $50,000 per eligible gain and is claimed via a straightforward TFN declaration on your annual tax return. While designed to stimulate housing supply, the program expires in 2030, meaning timing your acquisition within these LGA boundaries can yield meaningful long-term tax efficiency. ### Frequently Asked Questions

**Does capital gains tax apply if I sell my property at a loss?**
No, you do not pay CGT when realising a capital loss, but you can use that loss to offset other capital gains in the same or future income years. The ATO requires you to record the loss accurately and carry it forward on your tax return until it is fully utilised against future taxable gains.

**Can I claim a CGT discount if I own the property through a company?**
The 50% CGT discount does not apply to companies under current Commonwealth legislation. Corporate entities are taxed at a flat rate on the full capital gain, which significantly increases effective tax drag compared to individual ownership structures.

**How does the temporary absence rule affect my main home exemption?**
If you move out of your primary residence and rent it out, you can continue treating it as exempt for up to six years provided you do not claim another property as your main home during that period. The exemption only applies to the period you lived there plus the absence window; any sale after six years triggers CGT on the rental portion proportionally.

**What documentation must I retain to prove my cost base?**
You must retain purchase contracts, settlement statements, council rates, water charges, legal and conveyancing fees, stamp duty receipts, and detailed invoices for eligible improvements. The ATO requires these documents for six years after disposal, and missing records can force you to rely on conservative valuation estimates that increase your taxable gain.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**What is the CGT discount and who qualifies?**  
Individuals and trusts that hold a taxable asset for at least 12 months before disposal can claim a 50% CGT discount, meaning only half of the capital gain is included in your taxable income. Companies do not qualify for this concession.

**How does the primary residence exemption work?**  
If you’ve used a property exclusively as your main home, it’s generally exempt from CGT. Partial exemptions may apply if you rented it out for part of the ownership period, or if you moved out and purchased another home to live in.

**When do I need to report a capital gain?**  
You must include any capital gain (or loss) in your tax return for the income year in which the contract was signed and legal title transferred, regardless of when settlement funds are received or paid.

**Can I offset capital losses against other income?**  
No. Capital losses can only be carried forward to offset future capital gains. They cannot reduce taxable income from wages, salary, dividends, or business operations.
