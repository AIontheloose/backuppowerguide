### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do I choose the right fruit tree for my yard?**  
A: Start with your hardiness zone and daily sunlight hours. Apples and pears need cold winters to set fruit properly, while peaches and nectarines thrive in warmer zones. Pick varieties labeled “disease-resistant” if you’re new to this—they’ll save you time and spray cans. I always recommend talking to a local nursery; they’ll tell you exactly what thrives within five miles of your house.

**Q: Do I need two trees to get fruit?**  
A: Not necessarily. Many modern apple, peach, and plum varieties are self-fertile, meaning one tree will produce on its own. If you’re planting a traditional apple or sweet cherry, yes—cross-pollination helps. But for backyard growers, sticking to self-fruiting cultivars removes the guesswork and maximizes your limited space.

**Q: What’s the best time of year to plant?**  
A: Dormant season is your best friend. Plant bare-root trees in late winter or early spring before buds break, and potted trees anytime during the growing season as long as you water deeply. Avoid planting in the heat of summer or when the ground is frozen. Trees need cool, moist soil to establish roots before the stress of fruiting begins.

**Q: How much space do fruit trees actually need?**  
A: It depends on the rootstock. Standard trees can reach 20+ feet and need a large yard, but most home growers should choose dwarf or semi-dwarf varieties. These stay 8–12 feet tall, begin producing in just two to three years, and are perfect for smaller yards or even large containers. Space them at least 10–15 feet apart for airflow and future growth.

**Q: How do I keep pests off my trees without harsh chemicals?**  
A: Start with healthy soil—mulch, compost, and proper watering build natural resilience. Encourage beneficial insects like ladybugs and lacewings by planting companion flowers nearby. Use physical barriers like tree collars to stop borers, and wrap trunks in winter guards to prevent rodent damage. If you must intervene, opt for dormant oil sprays or neem oil applied early in the season before pests establish.
