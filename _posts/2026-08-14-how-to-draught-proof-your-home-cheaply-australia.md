### Step 1: Locate the Leaks Before You Buy

I always tell my readers to measure twice and cut once. Start with a simple draft assessment checklist so you know exactly where to focus your budget:
- **The incense or candle test:** On a windy day, slowly move an unlit incense stick or lit candle around door jambs, window frames, skirting boards, and electrical outlets. Watch for the smoke or flame flickering violently inward or outward.
- **Visual inspection:** Look for daylight peeking through gaps, cracked aluminium seals, or compressed foam that has hardened over time.
- **Floor & ceiling checks:** Gaps around floorboards, architraves, and light fixtures are common culprits in older Australian homes.
- **Thermal camera apps:** If you have a smartphone with a FLIR One or Seek Thermal attachment, scan your walls and windows for cold/hot spots. It’s surprisingly accurate and takes minutes.

### Step 3: Apply Weather‑Stripping & Door Sweeps

This is where the magic happens, and it’s surprisingly satisfying. 
1. **Measure accurately:** Use a steel tape to record the perimeter of each door and window frame. Add 5% for waste.
2. **Prep the surface:** Wipe down aluminium or timber frames with methylated spirits or rubbing alcohol. Let it dry completely; adhesive fails on dust or old sealant residue.
3. **Peel & press:** Start at the top corner of the frame, peel back the backing, and press the foam strip firmly into place. Work in 30‑cm sections to avoid premature sticking. Use a roller or your thumb to ensure even compression.
4. **Install the door sweep:** For gaps larger than 10 mm under internal doors, screw the sweep into the centre channel of the door bottom. Test the swing—it should close smoothly without dragging on the carpet or timber floor.

### Step 4: Seal Electrical Outlets & Skirting Boards

Electrical boxes are notorious cold bridges in Australian homes. 
1. **Turn off the power:** Switch off the relevant circuit at your switchboard. Safety first, always.
2. **Remove the faceplate:** Unscrew gently and pull it out just enough to access the gap behind.
3. **Apply low‑expansion foam:** Spray a thin bead around the perimeter of the box. Overfilling can crack the drywall or push the plate outward. Let it cure for 10 minutes, trim excess with a utility knife, and replace the faceplate.
4. **Skirting board gaps:** For gaps between timber skirting and brick/concrete floors, use a paintable silicone sealant rather than expanding foam. It remains flexible during seasonal house movement and won’t crack when painted over.

For those tricky electrical outlet gaps, a single can of [foam insulation sealant spray](https://www.amazon.com.au/s?k=foam+insulation+sealant+500ml&tag=owlno-22) is usually enough for an average three‑bedroom home. ### Step 5: Layer Up with Thermal Curtains & Draft Excluders

Sealing gaps is your first line of defence, but thermal curtains act as a secondary insulating layer. 
1. **Mount high and wide:** Install curtain brackets at least 10 cm above the window frame and extend them 15 cm past the sides. This prevents warm/cool air from bypassing the fabric.
2. **Choose heavy‑weave fabrics:** Look for triple‑weave or velvet blends with a thermal backing. They block solar gain in summer and retain heat in winter.
3. **Add a draft excluder roll:** For sliding doors or French windows where seals wear down, a felt or rubber draft excluder roll pressed into the bottom track stops air migration without permanent modification.

### Maintenance & Safety Checklist

Draught proofing isn’t a “fit and forget” job. Australian weather cycles through humidity, UV exposure, and seasonal expansion that wear down seals faster than you’d expect.
- **Inspect annually:** Check adhesive strips every 12–18 months. Replace if they’re brittle, peeling, or compressed flat.
- **Ventilation balance:** Sealing too tightly can trap moisture and encourage mould in coastal areas. Keep exhaust fans running during showers and cooking, and crack a window daily for cross‑ventilation.
- **Paint compatibility:** Only paint over silicone or latex sealants designed for topcoating. Never paint over expanding foam—it will absorb water and degrade.
- **Rental considerations:** Use removable draft excluders and magnetic weather strips if you can’t drill or adhesive permanently. Landlords often approve these as they’re reversible.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use standard household caulk instead of specialised insulation sealant?**
Standard acrylic caulk works beautifully for sealing small, static gaps around timber skirting or architraves, but it lacks the flexibility and thermal resistance needed for dynamic areas like window frames or electrical boxes. For gaps wider than 5 mm, low‑expansion polyurethane foam provides superior air and moisture blocking. Always choose products rated for interior use and check that they’re compatible with your wall material, whether that’s plasterboard, brick, or rendered concrete.

**How long does a complete draught‑proofing job take?**
A typical Australian three‑bedroom home takes between 3–5 hours to seal thoroughly if you work methodically. The first hour is always spent locating leaks and clearing debris, which saves time later. Weather‑stripping doors and windows usually takes 90 minutes, while sealing outlets and skirting boards adds another hour. If you’re installing thermal curtains or floor seals, factor in an extra couple of hours for measuring and mounting. Break the job into one room per weekend to avoid fatigue and ensure quality results.

**Will draught proofing void my home insurance or building warranty?**
Absolutely not, as long as you’re working on non‑structural elements like door frames, window seals, and skirting boards. Insurance policies only care about unauthorised structural alterations, electrical tampering, or use of non‑compliant flammable materials in ceiling cavities. Always follow the manufacturer’s instructions for sealants and adhesives, keep receipts for your materials, and if you’re unsure about working near live wiring, hire a licensed electrician to remove faceplates safely.

**How quickly will I notice a difference in my energy bills?**

If you use a smart meter, monitor usage during peak hours; that's where retaining heat delivers the biggest return.

**Can I draught proof if I rent?**
Yes! Focus on removable solutions like draft excluders, weatherstripping tape, and door snakes. These provide significant savings without permanent alteration. Always check your tenancy agreement for consent requirements, and offer to reinstate changes at the end of your lease to keep your landlord happy.

**What's the best time of year to start?**
Autumn is ideal, but spring works too. Avoid applying sealants in freezing temperatures or direct sunlight, as this can affect adhesion. Start early enough to enjoy the warmth before winter peaks!
