## Best Road Trips from Melbourne in 2026: The Definitive Aussie Driver's Guide

With petrol hovering around $1.45 a litre across metropolitan Victoria this year and national park day passes ticking upward, planning a road trip out of Melbourne now demands more than just a map app. It requires a proper budget, a reliable set of tyres, and a healthy respect for our regional roads. I've spent the last decade testing everything from high-clearance utes to efficient EVs on Victoria's gnarliest bitumen and most unforgiving gravel tracks. The vehicles that survive these trips with minimal drama belong to drivers who check their fluid levels before turning the key and understand how elevation changes your fuel map.

In this guide, I'm breaking down three of Victoria's standout routes for 2026. We'll crunch the real-world numbers, expose the maintenance realities you'll face, and point you toward accommodation that won't break the bank or get booked out by a mob of campervans. If you're driving from Melbourne, these are the roads where your machine earns its keep—and where you need to earn its trust.

### Great Ocean Road: Coastal Cliffs, Winding Bends, and Real-World Costs

The Great Ocean Road remains the classic Victorian escape, but let's be honest: it's not just a scenic drive. It's a test of brake cooling, tyre grip, and driver patience. Clocking 243 kilometres one-way with a realistic drive time of 4 hours 30 minutes (allowing for traffic and photo stops), this route demands respect for its frequent blind crests, coastal fog patches, and heavy tourist congestion during summer weekends.

**Fuel Math & Costs:**
Don't let the sticker shock on that litre price fool you; with a full boot, passengers, and the constant stop-start traffic around Lorne, expect your average SUV to sip around 10 to 11 litres per 100 kilometres. For our 243-kilometre trek, that works out to roughly 27 litres, translating to about $39 at current $1.45/L rates. However, once you factor in towing a small trailer or driving aggressively up the coastal gradients, pushing toward that $43 mark for one way is entirely realistic. A return trip will set you back roughly $86 in fuel alone.

**Road Conditions & Maintenance:**
After prolonged wet spells, the bitumen near Anglesea and Lorne can develop shallow ruts and hidden potholes from drainage failures. I always recommend dropping your tyre pressure by 10–15 kPa when venturing onto unsealed detours toward Loch Ard Gorge or the Twelve Apostles, but remember to reinflate before hitting the Princes Freeway. The sustained downhill grades near Bells Beach bite hard on brake pads; if you're towing or carrying a full load, consider replacing your front brake discs every 60,000 kilometres rather than waiting for the squeal. Brake fade here is real, so use engine braking in lower gears.

**Accommodation & Cuisine:**
For a stopover, Lorne's waterfront lodges book out months ahead in peak season; aim your booking window for at least six months prior. If you want fresher air and easier parking, Apollo Bay is the move. Grab smoked fish straight from the local wharf operators and hit the cafes along the esplanade—just be prepared to queue on Saturday mornings.

### Alpine Way: High Country Ascents, Elevation Changes, and Fuel Math

Heading inland toward the Victorian Alps via the Alpine Way transforms the driving experience entirely. Covering 400 kilometres in roughly 7 hours one-way, this route climbs steadily into snow gums and alpine plateaus. The fuel cost rises to approximately $71 AUD per way for a typical SUV. That's not a typo—elevation gain and cooler mountain air genuinely increase fuel consumption by 8–12% compared to coastal driving. On the climb to Mount Beauty, expect your average to jump to 12 litres per 100 kilometres due to the sustained load on the engine.

**Road Conditions & Maintenance:**
The road surface is generally well-maintained, but expect long, sweeping straights that lull you into complacency, followed by sudden elevation changes near Omeo and Mount Beauty. In winter, black ice forms rapidly on shaded bends, and gravel sections near the Omeo turn-off can chew through alloy wheels if you're not careful with your approach speed. I always advise checking coolant concentration before heading above 800 metres; a simple 50/50 premix prevents boil-over on steep climbs and protects your water pump from corrosion. If you're towing a caravan, the Alpine Way's gradients will punish under-inflated tyres and worn shock absorbers in a hurry.

**Accommodation & Cuisine:**
Omeo's historic pub is a staple for weary travellers seeking a hot meal, but if you're aiming for the chalets in Mount Beauty, book well in advance for winter runs. For summer, consider camping at Lake Hotham, though sites fill by mid-afternoon. The cuisine here is all about hearty comfort; look for wood-fired pizzas and local game meats on menus in Omeo and Dargo.

### Wilsons Promontory Circuit: Bushland, Gravel Tracks, and the EV Alternative

Wilsons Promontory offers a completely different flavour of Victorian road tripping. The 200-kilometre circuit takes about 4 hours to navigate, weaving through dense eucalypt forests, coastal headlands, and unsealed fire tracks. Entry requires a national park day pass at $35 AUD per vehicle, and if you're staying overnight, mid-range lodges in Slaughter Bay or Tidal River run around $150 AUD per night.

Here's where the 2026 landscape gets interesting: electric vehicles are suddenly viable for this circuit, provided you plan your charging stops. A Tesla Model Y rental sits at roughly $120 AUD per day ($360 total for three days including insurance), while fast charging at 100 kW costs about $0.30 per kWh.

**EV Efficiency Breakdown:**
An efficient EV typically consumes 15 to 18 kWh per 100 km on this circuit, accounting for coastal climbs and unsealed tracks. For a 200-kilometre loop, you'll use roughly 35 kWh total. At $0.30/kWh, that's about $10.50 in electricity—shockingly cheap compared to the petrol equivalent of roughly $30 for an ICE vehicle.
