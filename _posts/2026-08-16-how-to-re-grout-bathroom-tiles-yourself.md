### Safety First: Gear Up Before You Start

Before we grab our trowels, let's talk safety. Old grout dust contains silica, and mould spores are no joke. In 2026, we prioritise health just as much as aesthetics.

*   **PPE:** Wear a fitted N95/P2 dust mask to avoid inhaling silica particles. Safety glasses are essential when scraping to protect your eyes from flying debris, and heavy-duty rubber gloves will save your hands from chemical irritants in grout and mould removers.
*   **Ventilation:** Crack a window or run your exhaust fan at maximum capacity. Good airflow helps the grout cure evenly and keeps fumes at bay.

## Your Step-by-Step Re-Grouting Guide

### Step 1: Clear the Decks, Scrape, and Banish Mould

Let's be honest: the prep work is where most DIYers groan. However, skipping this step is the number one reason re-grouted joints crack within weeks. You must remove all old grout residue, silicone, and mould before applying new material. If you leave even a film of grease or mildew behind, your fresh grout simply won't bond.

1.  **Remove Mould First:** Apply a commercial mould remover or a diluted bleach solution to any black spots. Let it sit for 10 minutes, then scrub with a stiff brush. Rinse thoroughly and allow the area to dry completely. This ensures the new grout doesn't trap spores underneath.
2.  **Scrape Out Old Grout:** Use a grout saw for small jobs or an oscillating tool with a carbide blade for efficiency. For joints wider than 2 mm, aim to remove about 3 mm of depth to allow the new grout enough room to cure properly without bulging.
    *   *Tool Tip:* If using a multi-tool, set it to low vibration mode to avoid cracking your tiles. Always wear eye protection.
3.  **Deep Clean and Dry:** Vacuum the tiles thoroughly to remove dust, then wipe down with a damp cloth. The surface must be completely dry before you mix anything. Moisture trapped in the joints can cause adhesion failure.
4.  **Measure Joint Width:** Use your tile joint caliper gauge to measure your gaps. This dictates your grout choice. For joints **< 6 mm**, you need fine-sanded or unsanded grout. For joints **≥ 6 mm**, use sanded grout for structural strength.
5.  **Masking Tape Trick:** For crisp edges along skirting boards or fixtures, apply low-tack masking tape just outside your tile line. You can peel this off after cleanup for a professional finish.

### Step 2: Choosing the Right Grout for Aussie Tiles

Not all grouts are created equal. In Australia, our bathrooms face high humidity and temperature swings, so material choice is critical.

*   **Epoxy vs. Sanded Cementitious:** For showers and splashbacks, I strongly recommend **epoxy grout**. It's virtually impervious to water and mould, making it the gold standard for wet areas. While a 10 L bag costs around **AUD $58.90**, it lasts significantly longer than cementitious options and requires no sealing. For dry area floors or walls with standard joints, a **sanded porcelain tile grout** (approx. **$24.90 per 5 L**) is a cost-effective alternative.
*   **Natural Stone Warning:** If you're tiling with porous natural stone, standard sanded grout can stain the stone over time. You'll need a silicate-based sealant compatible with your stone type to prevent discoloration.
*   **Building Code Note:** In Sydney, re-grouting does not fix a failing waterproofing membrane. If you notice water pooling behind tiles or damp patches on adjacent walls, consult a licensed plumber before grouting. AS 3740 compliance is vital for wet areas.

When mixing epoxy, consistency is everything. Grout should be creamy, not runny. Aim for a viscosity of roughly **250 cP**. Here's how I test it: perform a simple "drop test" where you let a small amount fall from your trowel; the drop should hold its shape for about **3 seconds** before spreading slightly. If it runs too fast, add more powder gradually.

> **Pro Tip:** Keep your work environment controlled during the first 24 hours of setting. The ideal room temperature is between **18–22°C**, and humidity should stay below **60%**. If you're in a steamy bathroom, run an exhaust fan or open a window to help the grout cure evenly without trapping

...moisture or causing uneven curing. Once your grout has firm-set but before it fully cures (usually 20–30 minutes in, depending on ambient conditions), it’s time for the haze removal phase. This is where patience pays off. Use a clean, damp sponge with minimal water—wring it out thoroughly—and wipe in diagonal strokes across multiple tiles at once. Rinse and wring frequently. Over-wetting now will pull grout from the joints. After the initial haze clears, wait another hour and do a final buff with a soft microfiber cloth. 

Epoxy grout doesn’t require sealing like cement-based alternatives, which is one of its biggest selling points for longevity. However, proper cleanup of excess material within the first 48 hours is non-negotiable. If epoxy skins over on your tiles, don’t scrub—it’ll scratch the finish. Instead, use a dedicated epoxy grout haze remover or isopropyl alcohol (70%+) and a plastic scraper. Never resort to harsh acids or abrasive pads.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid:**
- Mixing in small batches larger than 5 kg—epoxy heats up quickly and can flash cure
- Skipping the priming step on porous tiles or natural stone
- Grouting over uneven substrates (lippage will crack even epoxy grout)
- Rushing foot traffic before the full 72-hour cure

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I walk on epoxy grout the same day?**  
A: Light foot traffic is fine after 24 hours, but wait a full 72 hours before placing furniture or exposing it to standing water. Full chemical resistance typically takes up to 7 days.

**Q: Does epoxy grout need sealing?**  
A: No. Unlike cement-based grout, epoxy is inherently non-porous and stain-resistant. Applying a sealer will only create an unnecessary film that can look cloudy or attract dirt over time.

**Q: What if the grout starts to set too quickly in the bucket?**  
A: Stop mixing immediately. Epoxy’s exothermic reaction accelerates in warm conditions. Work in smaller batches, keep materials at stable room temperature, and avoid direct sunlight on your workspace.

**Q: Can I use epoxy grout outdoors or in swimming pools?**  
A: Only if specifically rated for exterior or submerged use. Standard interior epoxy will yellow, crack, or degrade under UV exposure or constant water immersion. Always check the manufacturer’s technical data sheet for “UV-stable” or “submersion-grade” classifications.

**Q: How do I fix a color mismatch between batches?**  
A: Dry-blend all bags of powder together in one large container before adding resin and hardener. Pigment dispersion can vary slightly by bag, so thorough initial mixing ensures uniform colour across the entire job.
