### What to Look For When Buying Your Next Smart Lock

Before we dive into the gear, let's establish a clear framework for your purchase. Australia's unique environment demands hardware that can handle more than just a gentle sea breeze. Our homes face intense UV exposure, high humidity (especially up north), sudden temperature swings, and occasional power fluctuations during bushfire season. When you're shopping, prioritise these four pillars:

1. **Climate-Resistant Hardware:** Look for locks rated for indoor temperatures up to 30°C and humidity levels reaching 90%. This ensures the internal circuitry won't corrode or short-circuit during a humid Perth summer or a damp Melbourne winter.
2. **Local Warranty Support:** Overseas brands often leave Aussies stranded. Ensure your pick includes a minimum two-year local warranty with service centres in Sydney and Melbourne. Break down what that covers: battery replacement, motor failure, and app connectivity issues.
3. **Residential Security Certification:** Don't just trust marketing claims. Verify BIC Level 3 certification for Australia, alongside international standards like UL-2202 or EN 1303 for extra impact resistance and pick-proof tumblers.
4. **Connectivity & Retrofit Compatibility:** Decide if you need built-in Wi-Fi, Bluetooth-only reliability, or a hybrid system. If you're upgrading an older home, retrofit deadbolt systems save you from replacing the entire door frame.

#### FAQ: Smart Locks in Australia – Quick Answers
**Q: Do I need a separate hub for Bluetooth or Wi‑Fi locks?**  
A: Only if you’re sticking with older Bluetooth-only models that don’t support direct cloud integration. The Ultraloq UL3 BT communicates directly with your phone via Bluetooth, while the Schlage Encode and August Wi‑Fi Smart Lock connect straight to your home router. No extra bridge required.

**Q: Which batteries hold up best in Australian climates?**  
A: Low-drain lithium AAs (like Energizer L91 or Varta Lithium) consistently outlast standard alkalines, especially in coastal zones where summer humidity accelerates terminal corrosion. Plan for 3–6 months of service life depending on activity volume and temperature swings.

**Q: Are smart locks secure enough for main entry doors?**  
A: Absolutely, provided they carry ANSI/BHMA Grade 2 certification, use encrypted communication (AES-128 or WPA2/Wi‑Fi 6), and include a physical override key. Digital access is convenient, but mechanical backup remains non-negotiable in Australia’s older housing stock.

**Q: Can I install one myself?**  
A: Most modern locks are DIY-friendly if your door matches standard Australian bore sizes (25–30mm) and hinge configurations. If you’re retrofitting into heritage timber doors, multi-point systems, or reinforced steel frames, have a licensed locksmith verify clearance and strike plate alignment first.
