When I'm advising beginners, I always push for value that survives the abuse of rocky jetties, sandy shores, and mangrove roots.

#### Tackle Essentials & Line Strategy
Your rod is only half the battle. You need a solid tackle box to keep your hooks and lures from turning into spaghetti, plus a sensible line strategy. A **3-tier Tackle Box with storage slots** runs about **$39 AUD**, which is a steal for organisation. For line, stick to **12 lb monofilament**. It costs around **$8 per metre** but offers the stretch beginners need to fight fish without snapping the hook or losing the rod. Braided line (like 20 lb braid) has zero stretch and high abrasion resistance, but it's unforgiving for novices; you'll feel every rock and lose more snags. Start with mono until your hook-set timing is sharp.

For hooks and lures, grab a **20-piece set including 2–4 mm hooks and jigs** for roughly **$15 AUD**. Size 2 hooks target tailor and bream; size 4 hooks handle smaller snapper and whiting. Always finish with a quality knot like the Palomar or Improved Clinch, and keep a pair of flush cutters to prevent line twist. ### Where & When: Hitting the Water in 2026
Australia's coastline is massive, but you need to start where the fish are abundant and the conditions forgiving. 

#### Top Beginner Hotspots
1. **Port Phillip Bay (Vic):** Famous for snapper runs. The bay offers sheltered waters perfect for learning casting accuracy and reading water. Snapper here fight hard, providing that adrenaline rush without the open-ocean risks. Watch for mid-tide movement near sandbars.
2. **Jervis Bay (NSW):** A nursery ground for bream and tailor. The calm inlet waters and shallow reefs make it ideal for practicing hook sets and line management. Tides shift rapidly here; always check a local tide chart before launching.
3. **Noosa Heads (QLD):** Where the river meets the ocean. Early morning casts along the sandbar edge work brilliantly for tailor and trevally. The current can be strong, so anchor securely or fish from the rocks at slack tide.
4. **The Great Barrier Reef (QLD):** For those in Queensland, the reef edges offer incredible biodiversity. Beginners should stick to the outer reef channels where kingfish and trevally patrol. Use a fish finder to locate drop-offs below 10 metres.
5. **Coral Coast (WA):** Western Australia's Coral Coast delivers world-class barramundi and kingfish action. The water clarity here is exceptional, making sight fishing possible once you've mastered your basics. Fish the mangrove edges at rising tide for maximum strikes.

#### Seasonal Peaks
Timing is everything. In 2026, data confirms the peak fishing season runs from **March to September**. During this window, water temperatures rise through spring and stabilise, triggering aggressive feeding patterns in species like snapper and bream. If you're a beginner, aim for late spring (September) when fish are fat from winter feeding and water temps are warming up, making them more active and easier to locate. Always monitor swell forecasts; anything over 1.5m requires shore experience or a proper vessel.

### Safety & Regulations: Don't Be the Statistic
Fishing is adventurous, but safety isn't optional. I've seen too many novices get in over their heads because they treated the coast like a backyard pond.

#### Licensing & The Law
Regulatory landscapes vary by state. In **NSW and QLD**, you must purchase a **4-week saltwater fishing licence covering all species** for **$70 AUD**. This is available via the state-wide online portal. In **WA**, the system differs with an annual "Fishing & Boating" card, while VIC requires a separate freshwater/saltwater permit depending on your target waters.

> **Pro Tip:** Never fish without your licence loaded on your phone or printed. Fines for fishing unlicensed can hit up to **$3,000 AUD**. I don't mess around with this one. ### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Do I need a PLB if I already have a smartphone with satellite SOS features?**
A: Smartphones are convenient, but they rely on specific carrier networks and have short battery lives that drain quickly in cold or distress situations. A dedicated PLB has years of battery life, is rugged and waterproof, and works globally via the Cospas-Sarsat system regardless of your mobile provider. Think of your phone as a communication tool and your PLB as your lifeline.

**Q: Is registering my PLB mandatory in Australia?**
A: Yes. Under Australian law, all PLBs must be registered with the national database (managed through Cospas-Sarsat). This links your device's ID to your contact details and emergency contacts, ensuring rescuers can reach your family immediately if you activate the beacon. Unregistered beacons cause delays in rescue operations.

**Q: How often should I test my beacon?**
A: Check your manufacturer's manual, but generally, you should do a quick status check monthly and an active functional test annually using the designated "Test" button. Never press the emergency button unless it is a real drill or genuine emergency. Most modern PLBs have an automatic self-test feature that alerts you if the battery is low or needs replacement.

**Q: Can I use a PLB on a boat?**
A: Absolutely. While EPIRBs are designed specifically for marine vessels, PLBs work globally in open water. Just ensure your PLB is mounted in a bracket or pouch that allows it to float free if the vessel sinks. This ensures the signal transmits from the surface rather than being swallowed underwater.
