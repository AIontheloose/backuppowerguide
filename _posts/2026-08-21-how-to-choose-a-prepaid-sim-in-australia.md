## How to Choose a Pre‑Paid SIM in Australia (2026)

If you're staring at a supermarket shelf or scrolling through an app store looking for a prepaid SIM this year, stop. Don't let the marketing department fool you with headlines screaming "$15 for 1GB". That's a trap. In early 2026, Boost Mobile has already slashed their entry-level bundle to **$12 AUD** for 1GB. The old price tags are ghosts of last quarter's strategy. Worse, assuming that $12 plan will get you through a week in Sydney CBD is a rookie mistake. You'll be throttled to dial-up speeds before your morning coffee cools down.

I've spent the last six months testing prepaid plans across every postcode from Hobart to Cairns. The landscape has shifted. 5G is no longer just a buzzword for metro dwellers, but it

...isn’t a free pass if your plan caps speeds at 25Mbps once you hit the fair-use threshold. I’ve watched too many folks buy “unlimited” or “high-speed” bundles only to discover their throttling kicks in after just 3GB of heavy streaming. Here’s what actually matters when you’re picking a prepaid SIM in 2026:

**Read the speed caps, not the megabyte count.** A $25 plan with uncapped 5G speeds will outperform a $40 plan that throttles to 3G after 10GB. Check the fine print for “fair usage” limits and hotspot allowances—many carriers silently drop your mobile Wi‑Fi speeds to dial-up once you cross them.

**Match the network to your postcode, not your pride.** Telstra’s coverage still dominates regional and remote areas, but its prepaid plans carry a premium. Optus and Vodafone have closed the gap in metro zones, and MVNOs like Kogan, Amaysim, and Belong now ride those same towers at a fraction of the cost. If you’re chasing value, don’t sleep on the “big three” infrastructure.

**Track your top‑up rhythm.** Prepaid doesn’t mean chaotic spending if you automate it. Set calendar reminders for expiry dates, use carrier apps that send low-balance alerts, and avoid third-party resellers that markup plans by 15–20% just to move inventory.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Will a $12/$1GB plan actually work in 2026?**  
A: Yes, but only if you treat it like a burner line. Expect strict speed caps after 1GB, limited hotspot data, and no rollover. It’s fine for calls, SMS, and light browsing—not for streaming or navigation.

**Q: Does “unlimited data” mean unlimited high-speed data?**  
A: Almost never. Carriers use fair-use policies that throttle speeds to 25–100Mbps (or drop you to 3G) after a certain threshold, usually between 3GB and 25GB depending on the plan. Always verify the “high-speed” allowance before buying.

**Q: Can I keep my existing phone number when switching prepaid?**  
A: Absolutely. Porting takes 1–3 business days. You’ll need your current account details and a PAC or porting code from your provider. Never cancel your old plan until the new SIM is active and verified.

**Q: Which network should I pick for regional Australia?**  
A: Telstra’s prepaid plans still offer the most reliable coverage outside major cities
