### The Wash Routine: Saving Paint Where It Counts

The wash is where 80% of scratches happen. Follow this, and you'll be miles ahead.

**1. The Two-Bucket Method is Non-Negotiable**
Use two buckets: one for soapy water, one for rinsing your mitt. Always rinse your mitt in the clean bucket before dipping it back into soap. This prevents you from dragging grit across the paint. **Never use household dish soap.** I know some blokes do it because it cuts grease, but dish soaps strip protective wax and can leave a film that attracts dirt. In 2026, with advanced paint protection coatings being more common than a two-dollar dollar coin, using the wrong soap is just asking for trouble.

**2. Drying: The Overlooked Step**
After rinsing, use a large, plush microfiber drying towel to blot the surface dry. Never air-dry in the sun; mineral deposits in your tap water will bake onto the paint and etch permanently. Work from the roof down, folding the towel frequently to expose a dry side.

**3. Decontamination: The Clay Bar Revelation**
After washing, your car might feel rough even when it looks clean. That's road tar, industrial fallout, and brake dust embedded in the clear coat. This is where the clay bar kit earns its keep. Spray plenty of lubricant on a panel and glide the clay over the surface. You'll feel it jumping as it pulls out contaminants. It takes about 30 minutes per panel, but your car will feel like glass afterwards.

> **Pro Tip:** When claying, always keep the surface wet. If the clay starts to drag or change colour, fold the bar over to expose a fresh face. Never use the same side twice on a panel.

### Correction and Protection: Sealing the Deal

Once decontaminated, it's time for correction. For most daily drivers, you don't need a heavy compound. A swirl remover like the Chemical Guys kit is perfect for lifting those fine scratches caused by automatic car washes or improper drying. Apply it with a foam applicator pad in cross-hatch patterns, let it haze, then buff off with a clean microfiber towel.

For deeper scratches or if you've invested in a [Dual Action Polisher](https://www.amazon.com.au/s?k=Dual+Action+Polisher&tag=owlno-22), you can cut faster with less fatigue, but always test on a small area first. Follow up with protection. You have three main options in 2026:
*   **Wax:** Lasts 4–6 weeks. Great for deep warmth but needs frequent reapplication.
*   **Synthetic Sealant:** Lasts 3–6 months. Superior UV resistance, crucial for Aussie summers.
*   **Ceramic Spray:** Lasts 6–12 months. Offers the best hydrophobic properties and chemical resistance.

> **Detail Spray Guidance:** For weekly touch-ups, use a water-based detail spray for gloss enhancement without heaviness. Avoid solvent-based sprays on interior plastics or rubber seals regularly, as they can cause drying and cracking over time. Solvent-based sprays are only for removing stubborn tar or bugs from exterior panels.

### Under-Body Care: The Salt Killer

Coastal drivers in NSW, VIC, WA, and QLD know the drill: salt eats metal faster than it scratches paint. After washing the body, rinse the undercarriage thoroughly with a jet nozzle. Focus on sills, wheel wells, and brake components. Corrosion here can compromise structural integrity long before your paint fades. ### Interior Detailing and Maintenance Schedule

A detail isn't complete without the interior. Use your vacuum for carpets and crevices, then wipe dashboards and leather with 3M microfiber towels. Cotton rags can leave lint and abrasives; these towels are dense enough to trap dirt but soft enough for sensitive surfaces. Don't forget the "quick touch-up" detail spray for between deep cleans.

**Recommended Maintenance Schedule:**
*   **Every 2 Weeks:** Wash using two-bucket method; dry thoroughly.
*   **Monthly:** Interior vacuum and wipe-down; ceramic spray top-up if applicable.
*   **Quarterly:** Clay bar treatment; check paint decontamination.
*   **Bi-Annually:** Machine polish or heavy swirl removal (if needed); apply fresh wax/sealant.
*   **Annually:** Inspect under-body rust; consider professional ceramic coating refresh if DIY coating is worn.

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1.  **Skipping the Rinse Before Waxing:** Residues left from washing can mix with your wax, creating a hazy mess. Always ensure the car is bone dry and clean before applying protection.
2.  **Using Old Towels:** If your microfibres are stiff or smell musty, bin them. They've lost their absorbency and are now abrasive sandpaper. Wash towels in hot water without fabric softener to restore performance.
3.  **Ignoring Water Pressure:** Using a high-pressure washer too close to paint or seals can force water past gaskets and etch the clear coat. Keep the nozzle at least 30cm away and use a wide-angle tip.
4.  **Washing in Direct Sun:** Heat causes soap to dry before you can rinse it, leaving streaks that require re-washing. Always work in the shade or early morning/evening.

### FAQ: Dan's Quick Answers

**How often should I use a clay bar on my car?**
You should use a clay bar every 3 to 4 months, or whenever you perform the 'bag test' where a nylon stocking over your hand feels rough against the paint. In coastal areas with high salt exposure or inland regions with heavy industrial fallout, you may need to clay every 2 months to prevent permanent embedding of contaminants that can lead to rust pits and clear coat degradation.

Technically yes, but it's redundant and often counterproductive. Modern ceramic coatings are engineered to be hydrophobic and self-cleaning. Adding wax on top just creates a hybrid layer that traps dirt, reduces the coating’s water-shedding properties, and can cause uneven gloss or hazing. If you want extra protection or warmth, use a dedicated ceramic boost spray instead.

**Why does my paint still look dull after washing?**
Most likely, you're dealing with water spotting or bonded industrial fallout. Tap your dry paint with a finger—if it beads like mercury, you’re fine. If it sheets or leaves wet spots, you need a decontamination wash followed by a quick detailer spray. Regular maintenance is cheaper than paint correction later.
