### 🚨 What to Watch Out For: Safety & Side Effects

Before you add a greens powder to your routine, please consider these critical safety points. I always advise my readers to prioritise safety over hype.

*   **Digestive Sensitivity:** Greens powders can cause bloating or gas, particularly if you are sensitive to FODMAPs or high-fibre inputs. Start with half the recommended dose for the first week to assess tolerance.
*   **Blood Thinning Interactions:** Ingredients like Vitamin K (abundant in leafy greens) and high doses of Omega-3s can interact with blood-thinning medications such as warfarin. If you are on medication, consult your doctor immediately.
*   **Heavy Metals & Purity:** Some superfoods like spirulina can accumulate heavy metals if sourced poorly. Always look for third-party testing (NSF, USP, or GMP) to ensure potency and purity.
*   **Pregnancy & Allergies:** While generally safe, herbal additives in greens powders may not be suitable during pregnancy. Review the full label for allergens like soy, gluten cross-contamination, or specific botanical sensitivities.

### FAQ: Greens Supplements in 2026

**Q: Can I use a greens supplement to replace my daily vegetable intake?**
A: No, greens supplements should never fully replace whole vegetables. Wholefoods provide complex fibre matrices and phytonutrient synergies that powders cannot replicate. Use greens powders as a nutritional safety net on days when you miss your five-a-day, but prioritise fresh produce for optimal digestive health and satiety.

**Q: What is the best time of day to take a greens powder?**
A: Most experts recommend taking greens supplements in the morning on an empty stomach to maximise nutrient absorption and kickstart your metabolism. However, if you experience stomach upset, taking them with a light meal or before a workout can be effective. Consistency matters more than timing, so choose a schedule that fits your routine.

**Q: Are greens powders safe for people with kidney issues?**
A: Caution is required. Greens powders often contain high levels of potassium, vitamin K, and oxalates, which can strain compromised kidneys or affect blood clotting. If you have chronic kidney disease or are on dialysis, you must consult your nephrologist before using any greens supplement to avoid dangerous electrolyte imbalances.

**Q: How do I choose a greens powder if I have a sensitive stomach?**
A: Look for products that explicitly state they are low-FODMAP and contain digestive enzymes like amylase or lipase to aid breakdown. Avoid powders with high amounts of inulin or chicory root, which can trigger bloating. Garden of Life and Orgain often formulate with gut-friendly ingredients, but always check the label for specific allergens and start with a half-dose to test tolerance.
