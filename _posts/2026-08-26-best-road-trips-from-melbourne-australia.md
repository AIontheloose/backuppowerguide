## Best Road Trips from Melbourne in 2026

There is nothing quite like dropping the convertible top on a crisp Victorian morning, feeling the salt spray roll off the Great Ocean Road 2026 coastline, and letting the Pacific roar drown out the city traffic. But as any seasoned driver knows, the magic of a getaway lives or dies in the planning phase. If you are mapping out Melbourne road trips this year, you need to know exactly what your vehicle will cost to run, which seals hold up after heavy rain, and where to park without circling for forty minutes. I have been tracking 2026 fuel prices Victoria wide, testing suspension loads on gravel fire trails, and speaking with roadside crews across the state. The numbers are honest, the roads are mostly well-kept, and your options are wider than ever. Let us break down the four best routes you can tackle from Melbourne right now, complete with realistic budgets, seasonal timing, and maintenance-savvy advice.

---

### 1. Great Ocean Road – The Iconic Coastal Drive

**Route & Mileage:** ~350 km round trip | Melbourne → Geelong → Torquay → Lorne → Apollo Bay → Port Campbell → Princes Highway return  
**Road Conditions Checklist:** Mostly sealed dual-carriageway to Anglesea, narrowing to single-lane curves past Lorne. Wildlife crossing signs are active between 6–8 am and 5–7 pm. Speed limits drop to 80 km/h on coastal bends; watch for sudden fog patches near Cape Otway. Check VicRoads alerts for seasonal landslide closures near Aireys Inlet.  
**What I’ve Found:** Park at Loch Ard Gorge before 9 am or after 4 pm to avoid the coach tour crush. The Twelve Apostles Visitor Centre car park is free but fills by 10:30 am; head to Gibson Steps instead for better light and space. Grab a flat white at Apollo Bay Bakery and time your sunset drive along the Princes Highway back to town when traffic thins.  
**Where to Stay:** Budget: Otway Ranges Caravan Park ($45/night); Mid-range: Lorne Central Hotel ($180/night); Luxury: Cape Otley Resort & Spa ($320/night).  
**Best Time & Events:** Spring (Sept–Nov) for coastal wildflowers and mild winds. Autumn (Mar–May) offers clearer skies and fewer crowds. The Great Ocean Road Festival runs in July with live music and local craft markets along the coast.  
**Green Travel Note:** Stick to sealed pull-offs, use a reusable water bottle refill station at Apollo Bay, and avoid idling at lookouts to cut emissions.

### 2. Grampians National Park Drive – Rugged Granite Escape

**Route & Mileage:** ~380 km loop | Melbourne → Henty Highway → Wimmera Highway → Halls Gap park circuit → return  
**Road Conditions Checklist:** Main highways are sealed and well-graded, but secondary access roads to gorges can be loose gravel. After overnight rain, gravel sections become slick; reduce speed by 20% and avoid sudden braking. Wildlife crossings (kangaroos, wombats) peak at dusk. Park entry requires a valid vehicle pass.  
**What I’ve Found:** The Pinnacle car park fills rapidly; leave by 7:30 am for an un

interrupted parking spot and a quieter start to your hike. The Grampians’ sandstone peaks shift color with the light—warm gold at sunrise, deep violet by late afternoon. Fuel up in Warracknabeal or Halls Gap before entering; services are sparse beyond the main township. Carry a physical map as mobile coverage drops past MacKenzie Falls. For the best wildflower viewing, aim for late winter through early spring (August–October), but pack gaiters and sturdy boots—rocky tracks turn to mud quickly when the rains hit. Always register your vehicle pass at digital kiosks before you lose signal, and keep a spare 12V charger in your boot; battery drain happens faster than expected when running auxiliary lights and GPS in remote zones.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Are all scenic routes in Victoria suitable for standard sedans?**  
A: Most main coastal and highland loops are fully sealed and sedan-friendly, but secondary park roads, gorge access tracks, and outback spurs often require 4WD or careful low-range driving. Always check VicRoads and Parks Victoria road status pages before departure.

**Q: How do I safely navigate kangaroo-heavy roads at dusk?**  
A: Drive with high beams when possible (they increase visibility by up to 300m), scan the verges early, and reduce speed below posted limits in known crossing zones. If you spot a group, slow gradually—never swerve or slam brakes, which can cause loss of control on loose shoulders.

**Q: Do national parks require separate entry fees or passes?**  
A: Yes. Vehicle passes are mandatory for most state parks and cost around $36–$48 for 24 hours or $150+ annually. Purchase online via Parks Victoria or at entry kiosks, but keep a digital or printed copy as payment terminals frequently lose signal in remote areas.

**Q: What’s the best way to prepare for rapidly changing weather on remote drives?**  
A: Pack layers, a waterproof jacket, extra water (2L per person minimum), a first-aid kit, and a physical map. Inform someone of your route and expected return time. Weather in the Grampians and Otways can shift from sun to driving rain in under an hour—adaptability is non-negotiable.

**Q: Can I charge an EV along these routes, and are there reliable charging stations?**  
A: Major hubs like Apollo Bay, Halls Gap, and Great Ocean Road towns have public chargers, but sparse coverage exists between Warrnambool and Port Fairy, or past Stawell in the Grampians. Plan stops around known Chargefox or OVO stations and keep your battery above 30% when venturing off main corridors.

### Conclusion

Scenic driving through Victoria isn’t about ticking boxes—it’s about reading the road, respecting the land, and moving at a pace that lets the landscape speak for itself. These routes reward preparation over haste: check tire pressures, leave early to beat both crowds and heat, and treat every pull-off as a temporary home rather than a checkpoint. The state’s coastlines, ranges, and river valleys shift with the seasons and the weather, so flexibility is your greatest asset. Drive thoughtfully, pack light but smart, and remember that the best journeys aren’t measured in kilometres covered, but in moments felt—whether it’s the sudden break of sunlight over a granite outcrop, the quiet rustle of gum leaves at dusk, or the simple satisfaction of arriving somewhere exactly when you meant to. Stay safe, stay curious, and let the road unfold as it should.
