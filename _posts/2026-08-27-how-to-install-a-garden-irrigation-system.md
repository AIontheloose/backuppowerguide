### Why Install an Irrigation System?

*   **Water-wise gardening compliance:** Many councils now mandate efficient watering tech. Smart systems help you stay compliant automatically.
*   **Consistent moisture:** Drip irrigation kits deliver water exactly to the root zone, preventing dry patches and leaf burn.
*   **Time-saving:** Reclaim your weekends. No more lugging hoses or standing in the sun.
*   **Long-term savings:** Higher upfront cost pays off via lower running costs and plant health.

### 2026 Rebates & Grants: Don't Miss Out!

Before you buy a single pipe, check your local pocket. In early 2026, several state programs remain active:
*   **NSW:** The *Smart Water Rebate* offers up to **$500** for installing eligible smart controllers and pressure-regulating heads. Apply via the NSW WaterNSW portal within six months of installation.
*   **VIC:** The *Water Saver Rebate* covers a portion of parts costs for drip systems and rain sensor integration. Check your local council's website for additional top-ups.
*   **QLD:** Look for the *Smart Irrigation Grant*, which often pairs with drought resilience funding.

**Sarah's Tip:** Keep all receipts and install photos handy. Rebate officers love proof of efficiency!

### Step 1: Plan Your Zones & Soil Health

#### 1.1 Map Your Garden
Draw a simple diagram noting plant types, sun exposure, and slope. Group plants with similar **evapotranspiration scheduling** needs together. For instance, keep your lawn zone separate from delicate native shrubs.

> **Pro Tip:** If you're designing a serene Japanese garden feature, proper water management is key to maintaining that balanced aesthetic without over-saturating the soil. You can read more about integrating water features here: [How to Design a Japanese Garden in Australia](https://www.owlno.com/2026/08/24/how-to-design-a-japanese-garden-in-australia/).

#### 1.2 Size the Zones
Keep each zone **≤ 200 m²** to avoid pressure drop. Use a free online calculator or app to estimate flow rates. Label zones clearly on your diagram and physical valves.

#### 1.3 Soil Test & Drainage
Soil pH and drainage dictate emitter selection. A simple kit costs about **$30**. Sandy soils need frequent, short cycles; clay needs longer, slower watering.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I really need a pressure regulator?**  
Yes, especially for drip lines. Municipal supply often exceeds 30–40psi, which will split emitters and cause dry patches. The $19.50 unit pays for itself in reduced maintenance and consistent coverage
