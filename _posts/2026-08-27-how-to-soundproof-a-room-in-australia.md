### Measure Your Baseline First

Before you buy a single roll of vinyl, measure your noise levels. You can’t fix what you don’t quantify. Grab a basic decibel meter app or a dedicated SPL meter (around $40 AUD from local hardware stores). Place it in the corner furthest from the noise source and record readings at different times of day.
- 30–40 dB: Whisper-quiet library levels
- 50–60 dB: Normal conversation/office hum
- 70+ dB: Heavy traffic or loud neighbour noise (needs serious intervention)

This baseline will tell you whether you need basic absorption or full mass-loaded blocking. Tracking this also helps you verify your tax deduction if the upgrade is deemed a permanent home improvement.

### Step-by-Step: Building Your Soundproof Sanctuary

Ready to get tools out? Follow these practical steps. I've broken this down so even a weekend warrior can handle it. Remember, the goal is mass, damping, and sealing.

#### 1. Lock Down the Perimeter
Sound loves gaps. It sneaks through tiny cracks around doors, windows, and skirting boards like water through a sieve. In my testing, I've seen gaps let through up to 70% of noise!
*   **Action:** Take your 1 L tube of Acoustic Sealant ($30) and seal 100% of perimeter joints. Focus on where the wall meets the floor, ceiling, and around any fixtures.
*   **Result:** This single step cuts leakage by approximately 30%. It's the most cost-effective move you'll make.
> **Pro Tip:** Acoustic sealant is high-viscosity and stays flexible. Don't use standard silicone; it dries hard and lets sound back in. Apply generously in a continuous bead, then smooth with a wet finger for a clean finish.

#### 2. Add Serious Mass to Walls
Panels alone won't stop the bass from next door. You need mass. If you're renovating a first-floor room or a home theatre, installing extra drywall is your best friend.
*   **Action:** Install 12.5 mm Sound-Proof Drywall ($28 per sheet) on your walls. For the ultimate barrier, add a second layer using resilient channels to decouple it from the studs.
*   **Result:** Adding this mass achieves a 6 dB drop in sound transmission. That's a noticeable difference to the human ear.

#### 3. Apply Mass-Loaded Vinyl (MLV) & HVAC Notes
For existing rooms where you can't easily add drywall layers, MLV is your secret weapon. It's heavy, flexible, and incredibly effective. Also, never forget that sound travels through

...air gaps, ducts, and even electrical outlets. Before rolling out your MLV (apply it over studs or existing drywall with acoustic sealant at all seams), tackle the leaks first. Use non-hardening acoustic caulk around baseboards, window frames, door perimeters, and outlet boxes. For HVAC systems, line exposed duct runs with insulated baffles or swap standard registers for acoustically rated alternatives. Even a 1/8-inch gap can negate your entire investment, so treat sealing as non-negotiable infrastructure, not an afterthought.

### FAQ: Common Soundproofing Questions
**Q: Do acoustic panels actually stop noise from entering or leaving a room?**  
A: No. Panels absorb reflections to improve clarity and reduce echo—they don’t block transmission. For actual soundproofing, you need mass, decoupling, and sealing.

**Q: Can I soundproof an existing apartment without major construction?**  
A: Yes. Focus on weatherstripping doors, adding secondary glazing or heavy curtains, laying MLV under dense rugs, and acoustic caulk around outlets and baseboards. Targeted upgrades yield noticeable results.

**Q: Why don’t standard foam tiles stop bass?**  
A: Bass frequencies have long wavelengths that require dense, heavy materials to block. Thin foam only treats mid/high-frequency reflections. Use MLV, mass-loaded barriers, or dedicated low-frequency absorbers instead.

**Q: How do I know if my soundproofing is working?**  
A: Test with a decibel meter app or clap test before and after treatment. Look for reduced volume, fewer ringing tones, and less vibration in adjacent rooms. Professional STC ratings are ideal but not always practical for DIYers.
