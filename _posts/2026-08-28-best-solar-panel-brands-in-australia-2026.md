### Practical Installer Considerations

Before selecting hardware, you must understand the baseline performance metrics that matter on an Australian roof. The most common residential system size sits between 5 kW and 7 kW, aligning with daily consumption for homes running air conditioning and EV charging. Installers who chase wattage alone often miss critical factors like temperature coefficient and inverter compatibility. Once panels are mounted on a west-facing roof baking at 75°C, the **solar panel temperature coefficient** dictates your actual yield more than peak STC ratings. A difference between -0.29%/°C and -0.38%/°C can swing annual output by nearly 15% in Perth. ### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: How do I determine optimal string voltage for high-temperature installations?**  
A: Prioritise the inverter’s MPP range over Vmp at STC. In regions where roof temperatures regularly exceed 65°C, allow a 10–15% derating buffer on open-circuit voltage (Voc) to prevent early-morning clipping or DC protection trips during cool snaps after heatwaves.

**Q: Are bifacial modules advisable for coastal or inland sites?**  
A: Only if albedo and mounting height justify the cost. Inland desert sites with light-coloured ballast or gravel can see 5–8% rear-side gain, whereas salt-laden coastal zones often negate that advantage through soiling and accelerated frame corrosion unless specifically rated for marine environments.

**Q: Does a lower temperature coefficient always mean better real-world output?**  
A: Not in isolation. While modules with coefficients below -0.35%/°C retain more power at 75°C, they often sacrifice initial Wp or carry a premium that only pays back after 7–10 years. Calculate the levelised cost of energy (LCOE) for your specific latitude and tilt angle before committing.

**Q: How frequently should corrosion inspections occur on racking in humid zones?**  
A: Minimum biannual checks for fasteners, stand-offs, and junction box seals. In Queensland or northern NSW coastal belts, switch to marine-grade 316 stainless steel or anodised aluminium with polyamide spacers every 12–18 months, regardless of warranty claims.

**Q: Should I prioritise higher efficiency per panel or optimise array density?**  
A: Efficiency only matters when roof space is the constraint. For ground mounts or large commercial canopies, focus on cost-per-watt and thermal management. Less frequent replacements and better airflow typically outperform marginal cell-density gains over a 25-year cycle.
