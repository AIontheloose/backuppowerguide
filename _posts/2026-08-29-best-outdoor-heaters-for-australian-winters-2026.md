### Choosing the Right Heater for Your Aussie Winter

#### Assessing Your Space and Climate
Australian winters are wonderfully diverse. While Sydney might bask in a gentle 10°C, Hobart regularly dips below 5°C, and Melbourne's infamous four-seasons-in-a-day weather means you'll need flexibility. Start by measuring your patio or deck square footage. A 2-metre heating radius is perfect for intimate dinner parties or morning coffee spots, while couples hosting larger gatherings will want the 4–5-metre spread of a gas or propane model.

I always recommend matching your heater's wattage or kilowatt output to your actual space rather than overbuying. There's nothing more frustrating than wiring a powerhouse that only warms one corner while leaving your seating area freezing. Think of it like planting: you choose the right species for the soil, and here, you choose the right heat output for the footprint.

### Safety & Compliance: Don't Skip the Checklist
Safety is paramount, especially when we're dealing with open flames or high-voltage units outdoors. Australian outdoor heaters must meet specific safety standards to ensure peace of mind. Before you buy, always check for compliance with **AS/NZS 4341** for gas heaters and relevant electrical safety marks (RCM) for electric models.

**Safety Checklist:**
*   **Certification:** Look for the AS/NZS stamp on the unit or packaging.
*   **Clearances:** Ensure 1-metre clearance from walls, timber decking edges, and overhead pergolas.
*   **Stability:** Heavy bases are essential to prevent tipping in wind; never leave portable units unattended on uneven ground.
*   **Child/Pet Guards:** If you have little ones or furry friends, opt for models with cool-touch guards.

### Maintenance & Longevity: Keep Your Heater Happy
To get the most out of your investment, routine care is key. Here's a quick maintenance checklist by type:
*   **Electric:** Check power cords for fraying before each season. Wipe down heating elements with a dry cloth to remove dust.
*   **Gas:** Clean burner jets every 3 months and inspect hoses for cracks. Test the tip-over switch monthly.
*   **Propane:** Always replace the tank if the hose shows wear. Store tanks upright in a ventilated area away from heat sources.
*   **Wood:** Remove ash weekly to prevent corrosion. Schedule a chimney sweep annually if connected.

Expect a lifespan of 5–8 years for electric and propane units with proper care, while well-maintained gas models can last up to 10 years. Most reputable brands offer a 2-year warranty; keep your receipt safe!

### FAQ: Your Winter Heating Questions Answered

**Q1: What is the cheapest heater to run in Australia during winter?**
Electric heaters are generally the most cost-effective to run per hour if you have access to mains power and a high-efficiency model, thanks to their 100% conversion of electricity to heat. However, if your home has cheap mains gas connected, a gas heater may offer lower running costs for larger spaces. Always calculate based on your local energy rates and the size of your area to avoid surprise bills.

**Q2: Can I leave my outdoor heater outside all winter?**
It depends on the type and construction. Electric heaters with IP65 ratings and stainless steel gas models can withstand the elements, but you should always use a breathable, waterproof cover designed for outdoor heaters to prevent moisture buildup and rust. Propane tanks must be stored separately in a well-ventilated area, never inside a shed or garage, due to explosion risks.

**Q3: How do I effectively heat a windy patio?**
Wind is the enemy of radiant heat, so focus on blocking it before buying a heater. Install a solid windbreak like a bamboo screen or pergola slats positioned 1 metre behind the heater to deflect gusts without trapping heat. Choosing a heater with a directional reflector and angling it slightly down towards your seating area will also help direct the warmth where you need it most.

**Q4: Are gas heaters safe to use near timber decking?**
Gas heaters can be used on timber decking, but strict safety clearances must be observed. Ensure there is at least 1-metre clearance from any combustible material, including timber edges and overhead pergolas. Opt for models with "cool-touch" technology and a wide, heat-resistant base pad to protect the deck surface from concentrated heat and potential leaks.
