### My Go-To Recipe: Soft, Spiced & Perfectly Sweet

**Ingredients:**
- 500 g all-purpose flour (plus extra for dusting)
- 60 g granulated sugar
- 7 g active dry yeast
- 1 tsp mixed spice + ½ tsp ground cinnamon
- 250 g unsalted butter, softened (cut into cubes)
- 300 g premium dried fruit blend
- 2 large free-range eggs
- 180 ml warm full-cream milk
- 1 tbsp orange zest + 2 tbsp warm water (for glaze)

**Method:**
Begin by activating your yeast. Whisk it into the warm milk with a pinch of sugar and let it sit for exactly 8 minutes until frothy. What I’ve found is that room temperature eggs and milk are non-negotiable here; they help the dough relax rather than fight back against you. In a large mixing bowl, combine the flour, remaining sugar, mixed spice, cinnamon, and salt. Pour in the yeast mixture, add the eggs and butter cubes, and mix until a shaggy dough forms. Turn it onto a lightly floured surface and knead for precisely 10 minutes until smooth and elastic. Fold in the dried fruit blend gently so you don’t tear the gluten network. Place the dough in a lightly oiled bowl, cover with a damp tea towel, and leave it to prove in a warm, draft-free spot for about 90 minutes, or until doubled in size.

Punch down the dough, divide it into 12 equal portions (roughly 85 g each), and shape them into smooth rounds. Place them on your lined baking tray, spacing them generously so they expand without fusing. Cover again and prove for another 45 minutes. While they rest, preheat your oven to 180°C fan-forced. Bake the buns for 22–25 minutes until golden brown and hollow-sounding when tapped on the base. Immediately brush with a warm water and orange zest glaze, then dust lightly with icing sugar mixed with a drop of milk. Let them cool on a wire rack before sharing with your family over morning tea or Easter brunch.

### Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

Even seasoned bakers trip up when it comes to these classic rolls. Here are four pitfalls I consistently see, along with straightforward fixes:
1. **Yeast falls flat:** Using water that’s too hot (above 45°C) kills the yeast, while cold water stalls activation. Always test your liquid at exactly 40°C before mixing.
2. **Dense, heavy crumb:** Over-kneading develops too much gluten for a sweet dough, or under-proving leaves the yeast exhausted. Trust the visual rise, not just the clock.
3. **Soggy bottoms from fruit:** Adding wet, un-dried dried fruit floods the dough. Pat your currants and raisins dry with paper towels before folding them in.
4. **Cracked tops during baking:** Baking at too high a temperature sets the crust too quickly. Stick to 180°C fan-forced and rotate your tray halfway through for even colouring.

> **Pro Tip:** Always score the tops of your buns with a sharp knife or bench scraper in a cross shape before baking. This isn’t just decorative; it directs the expansion evenly so your buns rise tall without tearing at the seams.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I freeze hot cross buns for later Easter gatherings?**
Absolutely. Cool them completely after baking, wrap each bun individually in cling film, then place them in a heavy-duty freezer bag. They’ll keep for up to three months without losing their tender crumb or spiced aroma. Thaw overnight in the fridge and warm in a 160°C oven for 5 minutes before serving to restore that fresh-baked softness.

**Why do my buns turn out too sour or yeasty-tasting?**
This usually happens when the dough over-proves, allowing the yeast to consume all available sugars and produce excess alcohol. Always proof in a controlled environment around 24–26°C and punch down as soon as the dough doubles, not a minute later. If your kitchen runs particularly warm during an Australian spring, consider placing it in a slightly cooler corner or shortening the rise by fifteen minutes.

**Can I substitute dried fruit with fresh or frozen berries?**
I strongly advise against it for this specific recipe, as fresh fruit releases too much moisture during baking. That excess liquid breaks down the crumb structure and leads to gummy centres that refuse to set properly. If you want a seasonal twist, try adding ¼ cup of toasted macadamia nuts or a splash of rum-soaked sultanas instead for better texture and flavour balance.

**How do I keep the icing sugar cross from melting into the bun?**
The trick is timing and thickness, so wait until the buns have cooled completely before dusting them. Use a fine-mesh sieve to apply a light, even layer of icing sugar mixed with a drop of milk, which helps it adhere without sinking into the crust. Store your finished buns in an airtight tin at room temperature to preserve that delicate white cross until serving time.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can I make this spiced fruit bread ahead of time?**  
A: Absolutely. These loaves actually improve in flavour over 2–3 days as the spices and dried fruits fully meld. Wrap them tightly in beeswax wraps or airtight containers and store at room temperature for up to a week, or freeze for up to three months.

**Q: What’s the most budget-friendly way to source ingredients?**  
A: Stick to bulk bins at local health stores or farmers’ markets where you can buy exactly what you need without packaging waste. Purchasing whole spices and grinding them yourself also stretches your dollar while delivering far superior flavour.

**Q: Can I adapt this for dietary restrictions?**  
A: Yes. For gluten-free baking, use a 1:1 gluten-free flour blend and let the dough rest five minutes longer before proofing to allow proper hydration. For dairy-free versions, simply swap butter for high-quality olive oil or coconut oil—the spices will carry the richness beautifully.

**Q: How do I keep packaging waste low while baking?**  
A: Measure dry ingredients directly into your mixing bowl, use silicone mats instead of parchment where possible, and compost any fruit stems or spent coffee grounds if your recipe calls for them. Glass jars make excellent, reusable gift containers too!
