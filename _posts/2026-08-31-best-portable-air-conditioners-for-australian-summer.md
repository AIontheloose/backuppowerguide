### How to Install a Vent-Kit Safely & Securely

Getting your window kit fitted correctly is half the battle. Follow this straightforward guide to ensure proper airflow and avoid water leaks:

1. **Measure Your Window Frame:** Note the exact width and height of your sliding or casement window opening. Most kits accommodate 60cm–120cm widths.
2. **Assemble the Panel Kit:** Connect the aluminium panels using the provided clips and screws. Attach the adjustable exhaust hose to the rear outlet of the unit, ensuring it doesn't kink or stretch beyond 1.5 metres.
3. **Secure in the Window:** Slide the assembled panel into the window frame. Tighten the side expansion brackets until snug, then lock the safety latches. The panel should sit flush with zero gaps.
4. **Seal Every Edge:** Use the included foam weatherstripping or silicone caulk around the perimeter to block hot air ingress and keep condensation contained.
5. **Test & Monitor:** Turn the unit on for 10 minutes. Feel the exhaust hose—it should be warm but not scorching. Check the windowsill below for drips; a flat drain line prevents water pooling.

### Maintenance Checklist for Long-Lasting Performance

Portable AC units demand a little TLC to stay efficient throughout January and February. Keep this checklist handy:

- **Filters:** Vacuum or rinse washable mesh filters every two weeks. A clogged filter drops cooling output by up to 15%.
- **Condensate Drain:** Empty the internal tank daily during high humidity, or ensure the auto-evaporation system is cycling correctly.
- **Fan Blades & Coils:** Use a soft brush to remove dust from the evaporator coil and rear fan grille monthly.
- **Refrigerant Lines:** Inspect hoses for cracks or oil stains. Never attempt DIY refrigerant top-ups; contact an authorised service technician.
- **Battery/Backup Prep:** If pairing with a portable power station, check inverter compatibility (pure sine wave required) and store batteries in a cool, dry place away from direct summer sun.

### Quick FAQ: Your Top Portable AC Questions Answered

**Can I use a portable AC in a heritage home without damaging original features?**  
Yes, absolutely. Many modern units come with non-invasive vent kits that clamp onto sliding or casement frames using tension rods and foam seals rather than screws or drills. Always confirm with your local council or property manager first, but the Whirlpool and Daikin ranges are specifically engineered to be completely reversible and leave zero marks on historic timber or plasterwork.

**What is the best window type for installing a vent-kit?**  
Sliding sash windows and double-hung casements work most reliably because they allow the panel kit to sit flush while the opposite pane locks over it for security. Avoid arched or fixed-glass windows unless you invest in a custom-cut acrylic insert, as standard kits won't seal properly around curved frames, which defeats the purpose of proper ventilation.

**How do I interpret the EER rating when comparing models?**  
The Energy Efficiency Ratio (EER) tells you exactly how many watts of cooling you get per watt of electricity consumed. An EER of 4.0 means for every kilowatt-hour drawn from the grid, the unit delivers four kilowatts of cooling power. Always compare EERs at identical BTU ratings, as a higher number directly translates to lower peak-time electricity tariffs and reduced carbon footprint over summer.

**Is it safe to run a portable AC off a generator or battery bank?**  
It can be, provided you match the startup surge wattage correctly. Portable AC compressors typically require 2.5x their running watts for initial ignition, so a 1200W unit needs a power station rated for at least 3000W peak output. Use pure sine wave inverters to protect the circuitry, and keep the unit elevated off damp floors to maintain safe electrical separation during extended off-grid operation.
