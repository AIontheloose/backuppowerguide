## Planning a Family Camping Trip in Australia: The 2026 Field Guide

Let’s be blunt. In 2026, Australian bushfire activity has climbed by approximately 12% compared to the previous season, according to the latest AFES preliminary report. The bush is hotter, drier, and significantly more unpredictable than ever before. If you’re dragging your family into the wild without a rock-solid plan, you’re not adventuring; you’re gambling. And in this country, the house always wins if you underestimate the terrain.

I’m Jake Morrison. I’ve spent decades tracking weather systems across the Kimberley and freezing my nuts off camping in the Victorian Alps. What I’ve found is that family camping here isn’t about roughing it for the sake of suffering; it’s about preparation. It’s about knowing exactly where your water filter goes, how to read a red flag day warning, and

...when to pack up and walk away without hesitation.

The difference between a memorable trip and a nightmare comes down to three things: situational awareness, redundant planning, and the discipline to act early. I don’t sugarcoat it—out here, your family’s survival hinges on what you do before the first spark lands. That means mapping two exit routes from every site, packing a ‘go-bag’ for each person with masks, meds, and water, and running a quick fire safety drill with the kids at camp. It’s not paranoia; it’s parenting in the wild.

### FAQ: Family Bushfire Camping Safety

**What’s the absolute minimum I need to pack for bushfire safety?**
P100/N95 masks, a dedicated emergency go-bag per person, a reliable weather/fire app subscription (like your state RFS or RFDS), a satellite communicator or PLB, and at least 3 litres of water per day. Don’t skimp on these.

**How do I explain bushfire risks to young kids without terrifying them?**
Frame it as preparedness, not peril. Practice “stop, assess, and go” evacuation drills, let them pack their own “safety kit,” and emphasize that grown-ups always have a plan. Kids mirror calm; panic is contagious.

**Can I still camp during Red Flag or Fire Danger days?**
Only if your state explicitly permits it at council-approved safe sites. Never pitch up in dry bushland, near grassy gullies, or under overhanging eucalypts when the wind’s gusting past 40km/h. If the RFS says stay out, listen to them.

**What’s the biggest mistake families make when a fire approaches?**
Hesitation. Packing slowly, arguing about routes, or assuming you’ll outrun smoke in a sedan. You won’t. Have your car packed, doors locked, and windows up before you even see flames. Leave early, leave together.

**Are there safer camping zones during peak fire season?**
Look for established caravan parks with council-approved firebreaks, stone or gravel surrounds, and multiple vehicle access points. Avoid unmanaged national park campsites past March in northern and eastern states.

### Conclusion

Camping with your family under Australia’s open sky isn’t about avoiding risk—it’s about respecting it enough to manage it. The bush doesn’t care about your holiday calendar or your kid’s excitement. It operates on wind, fuel load, and patience. When you prep like a pro, pack like a survivalist, and plan like a parent, you don’t just survive the season; you thrive in it. Leave no trace, read the signs, and never confuse bravery with recklessness. The best memories aren’t made by camping where the fire came closest—they’re made by coming home safe to tell the story. Now pack your bags, check the forecast, and drive out knowing you’ve done everything right.
