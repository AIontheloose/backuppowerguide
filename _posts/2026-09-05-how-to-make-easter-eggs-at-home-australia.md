### Step‑by‑Step: Making the Magic

#### 1. Melt the Chocolate
Patience is key here! Use a double boiler (a heatproof bowl over simmering water) for gentle melting. If using a microwave, melt in 20-second bursts, stirring thoroughly between each to prevent scorching. Dark chocolate requires slightly more heat than milk or white varieties.

> **Pro Tip:** For an extra silky texture and a subtle flavour boost, stir in half a teaspoon of coconut oil or a tiny pinch of sea salt once melted. This also helps the chocolate set with a beautiful snap.

#### 2. Pour Into Moulds
Ensure your silicone moulds are completely clean and dry. Lightly grease them with a neutral oil like sunflower spray if your mould isn't non-stick. Spoon or pour the chocolate into each cavity, filling to about three-quarters full. Tap the mould firmly against the benchtop twice or three times to release air bubbles—this prevents unsightly holes in your finished eggs.

#### 3. Decorate While Hardening
As the chocolate begins to set (usually after 5 minutes), it's the perfect time to add flair. Use food-colouring pens to doodle patterns, and sprinkle sugar crystals for a glossy glaze.

> **Quick Glitter Tricks:** For a stunning "glitter rainbow" effect without clumps, mix a tiny pinch of edible glitter with exactly one drop of water on a small dish before sprinkling. This creates a liquid sparkle that spreads evenly. Dust lightly over the semi-set chocolate and let it dry for 10 minutes

#### 4. Unmold with Confidence
Once your eggs have set completely (give them at least 30 minutes in the fridge, or 15 if you're impatient like me!), it's time for the big reveal. Place a sheet of parchment paper on your bench and turn the mould upside down over it. Give the back of the mould a firm tap with the heel of your hand and wiggle it gently side-to-side. Gravity and that initial spray should do the rest! If any eggs resist, pop the entire mould back in the fridge for two minutes—the cold shrinkage usually helps them release cleanly.

#### 5. Storage Secrets
Your chocolate eggs are ready to shine! However, chocolate is notoriously sensitive to heat and humidity. Store your creations in an airtight container lined with parchment paper in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight. They'll stay crisp and fresh for up to two weeks. If you live in a warmer climate or are gifting these, keep them refrigerated but allow them to come to room temperature before unwrapping to prevent condensation from ruining that gorgeous finish.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: My eggs look cloudy or streaky. Did I mess up?**
A: Don't panic! Cloudiness is usually caused by moisture hitting the chocolate (make sure your mould is bone-dry!) or improper tempering. For a professional, glossy shine next time, try using couverture chocolate and following a proper tempering method. It makes all the difference in texture and snap!

**Q: Can I use regular baking chips instead of melting chocolate?**
A: While you *can*, I don't recommend it for moulds. Baking chips contain stabilizers to keep their shape in cookies, which means they won't melt smoothly or release cleanly from the mould. Stick to high-quality chocolate melts or couverture designed specifically for coating and moulding.

**Q: How do I fix a chocolate egg that breaks?**
A: No worries! Melt up the scraps and use warm chocolate as "glue" to patch it, or melt down all the broken pieces to dip fresh strawberries, pretzels, or cookie dough balls in the mixture. Waste not, want not!
