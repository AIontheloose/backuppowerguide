### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: Can I actually use my outdoor pizza oven during winter months in Australia?**
Absolutely, and many Aussies find it's the perfect reason to stay outside longer. While colder air can slightly extend preheat times, modern insulated chambers retain heat exceptionally well. I recommend using a windbreak or positioning your unit near an existing patio wall to block gusts, and always opt for dry, seasoned timber rather than green wood to maintain consistent flame temperatures without excess smoke.

**Q2: How do I clean my oven properly without damaging the interior?**
Never use water or harsh chemical cleaners on a hot chamber, as thermal shock can crack the refractory material. Once your oven has cooled completely, sweep out ash with a stiff brush and wipe the exterior with a damp cloth. For stubborn grease stains inside the dome, lightly sand the affected area with fine-grit sandpaper, then burn off residue by running a high-heat fire for ten minutes before letting it cool naturally.

**Q3: What's the difference between wood pellets and kindling for these ovens?**
Wood pellets offer consistent sizing and lower moisture content, which makes ignition easier and reduces ash buildup. Kindling and split logs provide that traditional crackle and deeper smoky flavour but require more frequent reloading and careful airflow management. If you're hosting a large group, keep both on hand: use kindling to establish the initial fire, then transition to larger seasoned pieces for steady heat during your baking session.

**Q4: Is it safe to bake dough straight from the fridge?**
It's generally safer to let dough rest at room temperature for 30–45 minutes before stretching. Cold dough contracts quickly when it hits a 400°C+ surface, which can cause uneven cooking or tearing. Additionally, refrigerated dough releases condensation faster, potentially making your crust soggy in the first few seconds of baking. Bringing it closer to ambient temperature ensures better elasticity and a more reliable rise during that critical initial bake.
