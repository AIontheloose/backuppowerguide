### Eligibility & Credit Score Requirements

Before applying, you must verify that you meet the issuer’s baseline criteria. In 2026, all major Qantas‑branded cards require a Good to Excellent credit score (typically 622+ on Equifax or Experian) and a minimum gross annual income of $30,000–$50,000 AUD, depending on the tier. Self-employed applicants or those with variable income streams must provide six months of bank statements or ATO notice of assessment. 

Lenders also assess your debt-to-income ratio (DTI). If your existing repayments exceed 30% of your gross income, approval becomes significantly more likely to be declined or restricted to lower limits. Approaching the application with a clean credit file and prepared documentation reduces the risk of hard enquiries impacting your score unnecessarily.

### Card-by-Card Analysis

#### 1. Qantas Premier Platinum
**Strengths:** Delivers the highest base reward rate among standard cards at 1.5 points per dollar. The 0% intro APR on purchases for 12 months provides crucial breathing room for large, planned expenses. The 10,000-point sign-up bonus accelerates early point accumulation.
**Weaknesses:** The $299 AUD annual fee creates a steep annual fee break-even requirement of approximately $20,000 in spend. It lacks lounge access or travel insurance coverage beyond basic emergency assistance.
**Who It Suits:** High-volume spenders who consistently clear their balance monthly and target premium cabin upgrades.

#### 2. Qantas Platinum
**Strengths:** The most accessible entry point at $199 AUD annually. Transparent 1.0 point per dollar structure simplifies reward rate optimization calculations. Includes basic travel insurance coverage for eligible purchases.
**Weaknesses:** Lowest earning velocity among the four options. No foreign transaction fees waiver, meaning international spend incurs a 3% surcharge. Sign-up bonus is modest at 5,000 points.
**Who It Suits:** Budget-conscious travellers who prioritise low carrying costs over aggressive point accumulation.

#### 3. Qantas Ultimate
**Strengths:** Industry-leading 2.0 points per dollar earn rate. $250 AUD annual airline credit provides tangible value for upgrades or ancillary fees. Substantial 20,000-point sign-up bonus after $1,000 AUD spend within three months.
**Weaknesses:** Highest annual fee at $499 AUD demands consistent high spending to justify the cost. No Priority Pass or airport lounge benefits. Points expiry remains a risk if annual thresholds are missed.
**Who It Suits:** Luxury travellers or professionals who can leverage the accelerated earning structure and credit without carrying debt.

#### 4. Qantas Business
**Strengths:** Competitive 1.8 points per dollar rate with $150 AUD airline credit and complimentary Priority Pass membership, delivering up to $200 AUD in annual lounge value. Ideal for claiming 100% of interest and fees as tax-deductible business expenses.
**Weaknesses:** $399 AUD annual fee requires careful cash flow management. Bonus points only activate on non-Qantas spend if you reach a $5,000 AUD annual threshold.
**Who It Suits:** Business owners and contractors who can fully utilise the tax benefits and lounge access while maintaining strict repayment discipline.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I calculate the annual fee break-even point for a rewards card?**  
Divide your annual fee by the average cashback or points value you earn per dollar spent on qualifying purchases. For example, a $95 fee on a card that earns 1.5% cash back requires roughly $6,334 in eligible spending annually to offset the cost. If you consistently fall short, downgrade before the renewal date.

**Can I carry a balance and still maximize rewards?**  
Technically yes, but the compounding interest will almost always devour any points or cashback earned. Treat reward cards as payment tools, not financing instruments. The math rarely works in your favor when APR exceeds 20%.

**What’s the safest way to manage multiple 0% APR promotions?**  
Only stack offers when you have a written payoff timeline and automated minimum payments in place. Set calendar alerts for promotional expiration dates and avoid new purchases until the intro rate expires, as balance transfer-only terms are common.

**Should I close old credit cards after paying them off?**  
Not immediately. Keeping older accounts open preserves your credit history length and expands your total available credit, both of which support a healthier utilization ratio. Wait 12–24 months before reconsidering closure, and always keep the account active with a small recurring charge.

**How do I avoid surprise annual fee increases?**  
Review your cardholder agreement annually. If your issuer has a history of hiking fees without notice, consider migrating to a card with transparent, capped fee structures well before your renewal date. Loyalty rarely outweighs predictable costs.

### In Closing

Credit cards are neither inherently virtuous nor dangerous—they’re financial accelerants that magnify whatever habits you bring to them. The difference between strategic wealth-building and slow debt accumulation almost always comes down to one discipline: intentionality. When you match card selection to your actual spending patterns, treat interest-free windows as temporary bridges rather than lifelines, and never let sign-up bonuses override principal repayment, these tools can genuinely fast-track your financial goals. In my years advising clients through credit optimization and debt restructuring, I’ve watched too many capable people lose ground chasing points or underestimating the silent compounding weight of minimum payments. Audit your cards twice a year, pay in full every cycle, and remember that true credit mastery isn’t measured by how many rewards you collect—it’s defined by keeping your balance light, your interest zero, and your financial future firmly in control.
