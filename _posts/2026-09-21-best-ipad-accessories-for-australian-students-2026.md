## The Stylus Trap: Why Model Matters More Than Marketing
### Apple Pencil 2nd Gen vs 3rd Gen – Pick Your iPad, Not the Hype

If you’re running an iPad Air or a Pro from 2018–2022, the Apple Pencil (2nd Gen) at $299 AUD is your only sane choice. It magnetically attaches and charges along the side rail. The 3rd Gen ($299 AUD) ditched that physical rail for a tap-to-pair dock design that only works with the latest iPad Pros (M4 generation onwards). Stop scrolling past this critical detail: if your tablet doesn’t explicitly support the 3rd Gen, you’re throwing money at plastic and Bluetooth pairing errors. I’ve seen too many first-years buy the wrong pencil because they trusted a product page’s “compatible with select models” fine print. Both sit at $299 AUD through Apple and authorised retailers, but only one will actually stick to your device without a third-party dongle or overpriced case getting in the way. For value-conscious students, verify your exact model number before clicking buy.

## Power, Protection, and the Australian Climate Reality
### The iPad Charging Brick You Actually Need

Your iPad doesn’t care about marketing; it cares about voltage stability. The **iPad charging brick** you buy in 2026 must support USB-C PD (Power Delivery) at minimum 20W, ideally 30W for Pro models. Cheap generics overheat and throttle output after three months. A proper GaN charger shrinks the footprint while managing heat far better than silicon-based alternatives. In Queensland and northern NSW, where ambient temperatures regularly exceed 35°C in semester one, battery degradation accelerates if you’re charging through a poorly ventilated folio or under a direct sunlit desk. Keep your iPad cool, keep your charge cycles healthy.

A **Durable iPad case** isn’t about looking sleek; it’s about surviving the 1.2-metre drop onto terrazzo floors in the engineering block. ## FAQ
**Does Apple Pencil compatibility depend on my iPad’s generation or just its model number?**
It depends on both, but the model number is the non-negotiable factor. Apple explicitly ties the 3rd Gen Pencil to M4-series iPads via a magnetic pogo-pin array and direct dock pairing, while the 2nd Gen relies on the side rail’s Lightning-adjacent charging contacts. If your iPad lacks the specific magnetic alignment strip or official compatibility listing in Settings > About, neither pencil will charge or transmit pressure data correctly. Always cross-reference your model number against Apple’s official support page before purchasing.

**Are student discount Apple accessories actually worth chasing at Australian retailers?**
Yes, but only if you know where to look and verify the terms. Officeworks, JB Hi-Fi, and Apple Education Store all run academic pricing programmes, typically offering 5–10% off genuine accessories during term months. The catch is that discounts rarely stack with bundle deals or third-party marketplace vouchers. Always request the discount at checkout, present a valid student ID or .edu.au email, and confirm whether the offer applies to styluses and keyboards separately. It’s legitimate savings, but not a blanket reduction across all categories.

**Why does my iPad keyboard keep disconnecting on campus Wi‑Fi 6?**
Most university networks in 2026 use band steering and aggressive client isolation to manage heavy lecture-hall traffic. Bluetooth keyboards can struggle when your iPad prioritises a congested 5GHz channel or gets throttled by network authentication gates. Switch your iPad’s Wi-Fi to manual selection, lock onto the “Uni-Student” SSID rather than “Uni-Guest,” and ensure AirDrop/Bluetooth discovery modes are set to Contacts Only during exams. If dropouts persist, switch to a wired USB-C keyboard adapter—it bypasses wireless interference entirely.

**Should I prioritise an iPad charging brick or a portable power bank for semester travel?**
Prioritise the charging brick if you study in one building; it’s faster, cooler-running, and cheaper per watt-hour. Choose a power bank only if your timetable involves cross-campus movement, lab rotations, or outdoor fieldwork. In 2026, look for USB-C PD 3.0 support, a minimum 10,000mAh capacity, and Australian Safety Approval (SAA) certification to prevent overheating in humid conditions. Never compromise on safety compliance for “ultra-light” unbranded cells—they’re a fire hazard and void your iPad’s warranty if they deliver unstable voltage spikes.
