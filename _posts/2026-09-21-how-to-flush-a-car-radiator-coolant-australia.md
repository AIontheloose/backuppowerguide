### Why This Job Matters More in 2026
Australian roads don’t care about your car’s thermal budget. Whether you’re crawling through the M1 on a Saturday afternoon or hauling a heavy caravan up the Mount Lindesay Range, your radiator is under constant siege. Coolant is a chemically balanced fluid designed to manage extreme temperatures and protect ferrous and aluminium components. Over time, however, those vital corrosion inhibitors deplete. The fluid turns acidic, eats away at thermostat housings, and leaves behind mineral scale that chokes narrow passages. I’ve stripped the radiator cap off six-year-old family sedans only to find sludge that looked like wet cement. A proper coolant flush restores flow dynamics, protects your water pump seals, and ensures your engine runs at the manufacturer’s optimal operating temperature—no matter how remote your destination.

### Step-by-Step Flushing Procedure
Work on a cold engine. Never open a pressurised cooling system when it’s hot, or you’ll suffer severe burns. Park on level ground, engage the handbrake, and place your drain pan under the radiator bottom hose or heater matrix pipe.

1. **Locate the Drain Point:** Most modern vehicles have a plastic petcock at the radiator’s lowest point. If yours doesn’t, loosen the lower radiator hose with a screwdriver clamp (or replace it with a quick-disconnect hose if you’re feeling clever).
2. **Drain Old Fluid:** Open the petcock or disconnect the hose. Let it drain completely into your pan. While it’s flowing, remove the radiator cap and heater matrix inlet/outlet

...hoses where accessible to break vacuum and speed up drainage. Once the old coolant stops running clear, close the petcock or reattach the lower hose securely.

3. **Initial Rinse:** Fill the system with distilled water through the radiator neck. Run the engine with the heater on max for 10–15 minutes. This circulates the water and loosens residual contaminants. Drain it again. Repeat until the runoff is completely clear. For heavily neglected systems, a dedicated cooling system cleaner (like Radiator Flush or Evans Waterless Cleaner) followed by multiple distilled-water rinses may be necessary.

4. **Refill & Bleed:** Close all drain points. Pour in your recommended pre-mixed coolant (usually 50/50 with distilled water). Start the engine, keep the radiator cap off (or use a spill-free funnel), and let it idle. Watch the temperature gauge and wait for the thermostat to open—coolant level will drop as air escapes. Top it up repeatedly until no more bubbles appear and the level stabilizes. If your car has a bleed screw (common on European models), crack it open until steady coolant flows, then tighten.

5. **Final Check:** Replace the cap or close the funnel valve. Squeeze upper and lower radiator hoses to confirm pressure buildup. Take a short test drive, monitor for overheating, and recheck the level once cool. Top up to the “Full Cold” mark if needed.

6. **Dispose & Clean Up:** Never pour old coolant down drains or into soil. It’s highly toxic to wildlife. Store drained fluid in sealed containers and take it to an auto parts store or hazardous waste facility for recycling.

### FAQ

**Q: Can I mix different colors/types of coolant?**  
A: Not recommended. Different formulations use different corrosion inhibitors. Mixing can cause gelation, clogging, or accelerated component failure. Stick to what your manufacturer specifies (e.g., OAT, HOAT, or IAT).

**Q: How often should I flush the cooling system?**  
A: Follow your owner’s manual, but generally every 30,000–50,000 miles or 2–5 years. Modern extended-life coolants may last longer, but neglect leads to sludge, corrosion, and water pump failure.

**Q: What if my thermostat doesn’t open during the flush?**  
A: The heater core may be restricting flow, or the thermostat could be stuck closed. Tap it lightly with a rubber mallet while running, or replace it proactively—thermostats are cheap and often fail right after a flush due to dislodged debris.

**Q: Is a spill-free funnel necessary?**  
A: Highly recommended. It prevents burns, captures overflow safely, allows you to monitor air bleeding visually, and eliminates guesswork when topping off hot systems.

**Q: Can I do this without jacking up the car?**  
A: Yes. Most modern cars have accessible radiator drain plugs or lower hose clamps reachable from above. Jacking helps only if you’re dropping the subframe or working on tight underbody clearance.
