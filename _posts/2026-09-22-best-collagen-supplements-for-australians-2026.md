### Understanding Hydrolysed Collagen & 2026 Science

Collagen accounts for roughly 30% of your body's total protein, acting as the structural scaffolding for your skin, tendons, ligaments, and cartilage. Production naturally begins to decline in our mid-twenties, which is why supplementation has gained such scientific traction. However, not all collagen behaves the same way in your gut. Whole collagen molecules are too large for efficient absorption, which is why **hydrolysed collagen peptides** have become the gold standard.

Hydrolysis involves breaking down collagen proteins into short-chain peptides and free amino acids like glycine, proline, and hydroxyproline. This process makes the supplement a highly **bioavailable protein source**, allowing these nutrients to travel directly to your bloodstream where they can stimulate fibroblasts to produce new collagen matrices. Clinical research consistently indicates that a daily dose of 10 grams is optimal for delivering measurable benefits.

In 2026, the efficacy of these peptides relies heavily on sourcing and processing. High-quality products utilise grass-fed bovine sources or wild-caught marine species, ensuring purity without the contaminants found in lower-grade supplements. Furthermore, ensuring your product meets **Australian TGA compliance** guarantees that it has been rigorously screened for heavy metals, microbes, and peptide chain consistency. This regulatory oversight provides peace of mind for Australian consumers navigating the supplement aisle.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I take collagen on an empty stomach?**
Yes, for many people, taking collagen on an empty stomach is actually preferable. Hydrolysed peptides are highly soluble and small enough to be absorbed rapidly without requiring extensive digestive enzymes. Taking them before breakfast or post-workout ensures they enter the bloodstream quickly without competing with heavier proteins like casein or whey, which can slow gastric emptying.

**How long does it take to see results in 2026?**
Clinical trials consistently show that 8–12 weeks of daily 10 g dosing yields measurable improvements in skin hydration, joint comfort, and nail strength. Structural changes like tendon recovery may take slightly longer, often requiring 12–16 weeks of consistent use. Collagen is not a quick fix; it is a cumulative nutritional strategy that rewards patience and regularity.

**What is the difference between marine vs bovine collagen?**
The choice between **marine vs bovine collagen** ultimately depends on your specific health goals. Marine collagen offers finer peptide chains and is rich in type I, making it ideal for beauty-focused routines and skin support. Bovine collagen provides a broader spectrum of types I and III, which suits joint recovery, gut health, and connective tissue priorities better. Both are equally effective when sourced from reputable suppliers and dosed correctly.

**Do I need a supplement if I eat bone broth regularly?**
While bone broth contains collagen, much of it remains in long-chain gelatin form during simmering, which reduces absorption efficiency compared to hydrolysed peptides. Additionally, it is difficult to consume 10 g of usable peptides solely through broth without ingesting excessive sodium or caloric load. A measured peptide supplement ensures consistent dosing and high bioavailability without the guesswork involved in dietary estimation.
