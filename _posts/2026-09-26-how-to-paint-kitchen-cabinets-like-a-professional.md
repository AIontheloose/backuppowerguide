### Step-by-Step Guide to a Flawless Finish

#### 1. Prep Work: The Foundation of Every Great Upgrade
I cannot stress this enough: preparation is where most DIYers lose momentum, but it’s also where you win the long game. Australian kitchens accumulate invisible layers of cooking oil and humidity that will sabotage any fresh coat if left untreated. 

* **Strip everything down:** Remove all doors, drawer fronts, and hardware. I keep a labelled ziplock bag for each door along with a quick photo on my phone showing hinge placement. It sounds tedious, but it saves hours of guessing later.
* **Degrease thoroughly:** Mix sugar soap and warm water according to the tin directions. Scrub every surface until the rinse water runs clear. Wipe dry with a clean microfiber cloth. This step is non-negotiable for proper adhesion.
* **Lightly abrade the surface:** Grab your 120 grit sanding block. You aren’t stripping to bare timber; you’re just “keying” the finish to create mechanical grip. Sand lightly in the direction of the grain until the surface feels uniformly dull. Wipe away all dust with a damp rag or [tack cloth](https://www.amazon.com.au/s?k=tack+cloth+woodworking+dust&tag=owlno-22).

#### 2. Priming: Locking in Durability
Primer might feel like an unnecessary extra step, but it’s your insurance policy against peeling and chipping. Modern **low-VOC paint** options now comply with strict indoor air quality standards, so you can work safely without choking fumes. 

* **Select a bonding primer:** The PPG 2K Cabinet Primer is excellent for its moisture resistance and fast dry time. It bonds directly to sealed timber, laminate, and melamine without etching chemicals.
* **Apply thin, even layers:** Load your roller lightly. Thick coats lead to runs and extended drying times. Roll the flat panels first, then use a brush for edges.
* **Sand between coats:** Once fully dry (usually 2–4 hours), scuff-sand with 120 grit paper and wipe clean. This ensures the topcoat bonds like a second skin.

#### 3. Painting: Rolling, Cutting In, and Finishing
Now comes the satisfying part! I recommend Dulux Select Premium Semi-Gloss for its durability and light-reflecting properties, which instantly brighten smaller kitchens. 

* **Roll first:** Pour paint into your tray. Roll a thin, even coat onto the large flat panels using a fresh foam roller cover. This minimises texture and lays down a smooth base.
* **Cut in with the brush:** Switch to your Jenson brush for frames, corners, and recessed panels. The dual-grip handle keeps your hand comfortable through dozens of doors.
* **Tip off for that spray-like look:** Immediately after rolling a section, lightly drag the dry tip of your brush over the wet paint in long, downward strokes. This smooths out roller stipple and creates a seamless transition. Keep your workspace well-ventilated but avoid direct drafts, which can cause premature cracking. #### 4. The Protective Topcoat
For kitchens in humid climates or those near open-plan living areas, a clear topcoat adds serious longevity. A single coat of Sherwin-Williams Clear Gloss enhances depth and creates a wipeable barrier against steam and splashes. Apply this only after your second paint coat has fully cured (usually 48 hours).

#### 5. Reassembly & Long-Term Care
Once everything is bone dry, rehang the doors using your photos and labels as reference. Tighten screws gradually to avoid cross-threading. For daily maintenance, stick to a damp cloth with a drop of mild detergent. Avoid abrasive pads or ammonia-based cleaners, which will dull your finish over time. ### Frequently Asked Questions

**1. How long will it actually take to complete this project?**
Patience is your greatest ally here. A standard kitchen refresh typically requires three to five days from start to finish, depending on ambient humidity and temperature. This timeline includes thorough degreasing, light sanding, two primer coats, two paint coats, and necessary drying intervals between each layer. Rushing the curing process is the most common reason DIY finishes end up sticky or uneven, so build buffer days into your schedule rather than trying to power through.

**2. Can I successfully paint laminate or melamine cabinets?**
Absolutely, but adhesion requires a slightly different approach. Laminate and melamine are non-porous by design, which means standard paints will simply sit on top and peel once you open a drawer. That’s why a dedicated bonding primer like the PPG 2K option is completely non-negotiable for these surfaces. You must still lightly abrade the factory gloss with 120 grit paper to create microscopic keying points before applying any coating. Once primed correctly, these cabinets take paint beautifully and can last for years.

**3. Is it worth buying a paint sprayer instead of using brushes and rollers?**
For most homeowners, a sprayer introduces more problems than it solves. While professional finish sprayers deliver an incredibly smooth result, they require extensive masking, heavy ventilation, and significant cleanup time that often outweighs the benefits for weekend DIYers. Brushes and foam rollers, when used with the tip-off technique described above, produce nearly identical results with far less mess. If you do decide to rent a sprayer, invest heavily in drop cloths and tape, and work in a controlled outdoor environment.

**4. How do I ensure my painted cabinets won’t chip or scratch over time?**
Durability comes down to three factors: proper surface prep, quality topcoat application, and gentle daily maintenance. Always apply paint in thin layers rather than thick globs, as thick films remain flexible and prone to cracking when wood expands with seasonal temperature shifts. Allow full cure time (usually seven days) before subjecting the cabinets to heavy use or cleaning agents. Finally, install soft-close hinges and drawer slides to minimise physical impact on the painted edges, which are the most vulnerable areas during daily operation.
