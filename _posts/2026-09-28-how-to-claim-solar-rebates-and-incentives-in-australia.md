## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do I verify my eligibility for 2026 solar rebates?**  
A: Start at the Clean Energy Regulator’s updated Eligibility Dashboard. Verification is now fully automated—you’ll need a valid electricity account in your name, proof of property ownership or landlord consent, and an accredited installer’s compliance certificate. Your post code determines which state top-ups apply.

**Q: Can I combine federal Small-scale Technology Certificates (STCs) with state-level incentives?**  
A: Yes. The 2026 framework explicitly permits stacking where legislation allows. Federal STCs automatically reduce upfront costs at point of sale, while most states now run parallel rebate programs that deposit directly into your nominated bank account within weeks of system activation.

**Q: What’s the typical processing time for rebate payouts?**  
A: Thanks to the unified Clean Energy Portal launched in late 2025, 92% of approved claims are processed within 14 business days. Delays almost always stem from incomplete documentation, mismatched meter configurations, or grid connection hold-ups—not bureaucratic bottlenecks.

**Q: Do renters or apartment dwellers qualify?**  
A: Traditional rebates require property ownership, but the 2026 Community Solar Initiative expands eligibility to multi-dwelling buildings and long-term leaseholders with landlord approval. Incentives are claimed through your building’s strata council or designated energy manager, then apportioned according to shared generation data.

**Q: How do I avoid common application mistakes?**  
A: Never skip the pre-installation compliance check, use only CESR-accredited installers, and verify that your inverter model is on the approved 2026 compatibility list. The majority of rejections occur because of expired accreditation certificates, incorrect meter type selection, or attempting to claim before grid interconnection approval.
