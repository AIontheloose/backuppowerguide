### Getting Started: Your First Steps
Before you list your first item, you’ll need a verified eBay Australia account linked to an Australian bank account and a registered address. In 2026, eBay has streamlined verification with biometric login options and instant ID checks through myGov integration, making setup faster than ever.

Once logged in, navigate to “Sell” from your dashboard. eBay now offers guided listing modes tailored to beginners: Quick Sell (for straightforward items), Standard Listing (full customization), and Bulk Upload (for inventory-heavy sellers). For new sellers, I recommend starting with Standard Listings—you’ll learn the platform’s nuances without feeling overwhelmed.

### Crafting Listings That Convert
Photography remains king. eBay’s 2026 interface auto-enhances lighting and background removal for first-time uploads, but natural light and a clean backdrop still win trust. Title optimization matters less now; eBay’s AI-driven search prioritizes item specifics, condition clarity, and fast shipping commitments over keyword stuffing.

Pricing? Use the “Suggested Price” tool, but cross-reference sold listings in your local category. Australian buyers respond well to transparent condition notes and realistic return policies. Free returns have become a conversion booster, not a loss leader—when handled strategically.

### Shipping & Payments in 2026
eBay Australia now integrates with Australia Post’s Click & Send API, allowing real-time label generation, automated tracking updates, and subsidized rates for new sellers. Dropshipping is permitted but must comply with eBay’s 2025 Transparency Policy—local sourcing or verified suppliers are strongly recommended to avoid performance penalties.

Payments flow directly into your linked bank account within two business days of confirmed delivery. No more waiting on third-party holds; eBay’s managed payments system handles payouts, fees, and buyer protection seamlessly.

### Maximizing Visibility & Avoiding Common Pitfalls
- List consistently: 3–5 items weekly signals an active seller to the algorithm.
- Use eBay’s Promoted Listings Standard (1–4% fee) for competitive categories.
- Monitor your “Seller Performance Dashboard” weekly—late shipments or high defect rates trigger ranking penalties.
- Avoid overpromising: 2026 buyers check delivery estimates against real carrier data before purchasing.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Do I need to register as a business to sell on eBay Australia?**  
A: Not initially. You can start as an individual seller, but once you cross $75,000 AUD in annual sales, the ATO requires you to register for GST and report earnings accordingly. Keep detailed records from day one.

**Q: What fees should I expect in 2026?**  
A: eBay charges a final value fee (typically 8–12% depending on category) plus a payment processing fee (~2.95%). Promoted Listings and store subscriptions are optional but can improve visibility. New sellers often qualify for reduced insertion fees during their first 100 listings.

**Q: How do I handle returns or damaged items?**  
A: Set clear return windows (30 days is standard). Use eBay’s Return Centre to generate prepaid labels automatically. For high-value items, require signature-on-delivery and photograph packaging before dispatch.

**Q: Is dropshipping allowed on eBay Australia?**  
A: Only under strict conditions. You must be the seller of record, ship within your stated handling time, and never use supplier-branded packaging. Local sourcing or wholesale partnerships are far more sustainable for long-term account health.
