## Hook

I was sitting on the side of the Eyre Highway in ’24, watching dust swirl past my ute while I waited for a tow truck that wasn’t coming. The lesson stuck: when you’re thousands of kilometres from the nearest service station, your boot is your lifeline. That’s exactly why I’ve spent the last decade tracking down which underbonnet checks actually save real money and which ones just pad the mechanic’s wallet. In 2026, with the average Australian car owner shelling out roughly $2,200 a year on routine upkeep, that figure isn’t just a statistic—it’s a warning. Fuel prices are stabilising, but parts markups haven’t budged. The good news? You don’t need a dealership bay or a factory-trained credential to keep your vehicle running smoothly. With a handful of basic tools and a willingness to learn, you can slash those bills while gaining a deeper understanding of what makes your car tick.

### Safety First: The Non-Negotiables
Before you swing a single wrench, treat every DIY session like a professional workshop. Always work on a level, solid surface. Never rely solely on a hydraulic jack—use rated jack stands under the manufacturer’s lift points. Wear safety glasses and nitrile gloves when handling fluids or grinding metal. Double-check torque specifications with a calibrated wrench; guessing on lug nuts or caliper bolts is how good intentions turn into costly damage. Your wallet will thank you, but your safety isn’t a bargaining chip.

### 1. Oil Change – The Foundation of Engine Health

**Why it matters:**  
Oil is the blood of your engine. It reduces friction, carries away heat, and suspends contaminants until they’re filtered out. In Australia’s climate, thermal degradation happens faster. If you’re running long commutes or towing a camper, pushing past the service interval risks sludge buildup and premature wear.

**What you need:**  
- 5 L bottle of 5W-30 motor oil – **$45**  
- Oil filter – **$17**  
- Drain pan, funnel, socket set, and crush washer – **$10**  

**Step‑by‑step:**  
1. Run the engine for five minutes to warm the oil, then shut it off and pop the bonnet.  
2. Position your drain pan under the sump plug and remove the bolt with the correct socket. Let it drain completely (usually 10–15 minutes).  
3. Unscrew the old filter by hand. Lightly coat the new filter’s rubber gasket with fresh oil before threading it on.  
4. Clean and refit the drain plug with a new crush washer. Tighten to spec—over-torquing strips threads, under-torquing causes leaks.  
5. Pour in 4.5 L initially, check the dipstick, and top up slowly until you hit the max mark.  

**Cost savings:**  
Doing this twice yearly instead of at a dealership saves roughly **$120–$160 annually**.

**Frequently Asked Questions**  

**Q: Can I use any oil filter?**  
A: Stick to OEM or reputable aftermarket brands (K&N, Bosch, Wix). Cheap filters often lack proper bypass valves or filtration media, which can starve your engine under stress and let sludge circulate right back into the pump.

**Q: How do I dispose of old oil and the filter?**  
A: Never pour oil down drains, onto soil, or in household bins. Take both to an auto parts store, council recycling centre, or service station—they accept used oil and filters for free. Drain the filter upside-down for 24 hours before recycling to prevent leaks at the facility.

**Q: What if I over-tighten the drain plug?**  
A: It’s a common mistake that strips the aluminium sump threads—a costly nightmare fix. Always use a torque wrench set to your manufacturer’s spec (usually 25–30 Nm). If it feels like it’s binding, back off and re-check the crush washer alignment.

**Q: Is synthetic oil worth the extra cost?**  
A: Yes for modern engines. It flows better during cold starts, resists thermal breakdown under load, and protects turbocharged or high-RPM components more effectively. The 20% price premium pays for itself in extended drain intervals and reduced long-term wear.

**Q: Can I skip the oil filter if I change frequently?**  
A: Absolutely not. The filter traps microscopic particles that bypass the oil pump’s relief valve. Even with freshly pumped oil, unfiltered abrasives will accelerate wear on camshafts, main bearings, and piston rings within hundreds of kilometres.
