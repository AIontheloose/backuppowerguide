#### Wi‑Fi Diagnostics: Stop Guessing
Before you buy, run a diagnostic. Use tools like **NetSpot** or the free version of **Wi‑Fi Analyzer**. Look for high jitter (variance in packet arrival time). If your jitter sits above 20ms on the 5 GHz band while sitting next to the router, your environment is noisy. Run a wired speed test via Ethernet first to rule out ISP issues. If your upload throughput dips below 50 Mbps or jitter spikes when another device activates, you have an interference or hardware bottleneck, not an ISP problem.

### Audio Clarity Without the Markup

Audio is what people remember. If they can't hear you, you might as well be a ghost. You don't need a $300 microphone to pull this off. Windows 11's built‑in AI‑powered background noise cancellation isn't a gimmick; it actively filters out dog barks, vacuum cleaners, and neighbourly lawn mowers by up to 90%. Turn it on in Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone > Background noise suppression.

If your laptop mic is still garbage, grab a budget dynamic mic like the Fifine K669B for around **$60 AUD**. The directional pickup pattern rejects room echo far better than omnidirectional USB condensers, which are just recording your reverberation.

#### Quick Fixes That Cost Nothing
Hardware only gets you halfway. Here are software tweaks that save sanity:
*   **Disable Battery Saver:** On laptops, battery-saving modes throttle the CPU and can kill webcam performance or introduce latency spikes. Plug in, or set "Optimal" power plan while on Wi‑Fi.
*   **Schedule Cloud Backups:** Never run a full system update or cloud backup during work hours. I've seen countless Aussies blame their ISP when the real culprit was OneDrive or Time Machine churning through your upload bandwidth. Schedule syncs for after 6 PM.
*   **QoS Prioritisation:** If your router supports Quality of Service, tag your conferencing device (laptop/phone) as high priority. This prevents a firmware update on your smart TV from killing your call.

### FAQ: Video Call Quality in 2026

**Is Wi‑Fi 6E actually necessary for video calls in 2026?**
Yes, if you have multiple devices active in the home. Wi‑Fi 6E opens the dedicated 6 GHz band, which is free from the interference plaguing the crowded 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands caused by neighbours' routers and smart home devices. This dedicated spectrum drastically reduces jitter and latency, which are the primary causes of choppy video and robotic audio during **home video conferencing**.

**What is the best budget microphone for clear audio clarity?**
The Fifine K669B dynamic microphone remains the value king at around $60 AUD. Unlike cheap condenser mics that pick up keyboard clacks and room echo, this dynamic mic focuses on your voice directionally. It requires no phantom power and plugs straight into USB, offering professional-grade rejection of background noise that Windows 11's AI can't fix alone.

**How do I test if my ISP is throttling my video calls?**
Run a speed test via Ethernet cable to rule out Wi‑Fi issues. Then, use a tool like `speedtest-cli` in your terminal during a call to monitor real-time upload stability. If your baseline upload is 100 Mbps but drops consistently when conferencing apps are open, check your router's QoS logs or contact your ISP. In Australia, some plans throttle specific application ports; a low-latency VPN can sometimes bypass this if port-based throttling is the culprit.

**Can I use my phone as a primary device for work calls?**
You can, but not without caveats. While the Samsung S30's 5G connection offers impressive bandwidth, mobile plans often have "fair usage" policies that throttle video streaming speeds after a certain threshold. Additionally, battery drain on hotspotting is significant. Use your phone as a backup tether for critical calls rather than a primary workstation unless you have an unlimited business data plan with no throttling.
