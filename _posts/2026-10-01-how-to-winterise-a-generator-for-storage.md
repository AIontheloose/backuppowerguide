### Why Winterising Matters: Standards, Risks, and Climate Reality

Winterisation isn't just folklore; it's engineering compliance. The Australian Standard **AS 4770-2014** explicitly states that "generators shall be stored with fuel stabilised to prevent phase separation and engine oil replaced if storage exceeds three months." Ignoring this isn't just a maintenance gap; it's a warranty risk. Many manufacturers void coverage if stale fuel damages the injectors or if acidic byproducts eat through the crankcase.

Australia's climate diversity means your winterisation strategy must match your postcode. A one-size-fits-all approach leaves you exposed to specific regional threats.

| Climate Zone | Key Winter Threats | Winterisation Focus |
|--------------|--------------------|---------------------|
| **Temperate** (Melbourne, Sydney) | Moisture ingress, red dust, UV degradation. | Seal all intakes, use a breathable cover, check for bird nests in the exhaust. |
| **Alpine** (Thredbo, Berry, Cooma) | Diesel gel point, oil thickening, battery sulfation below -5°C. | Use winter-grade oil (0W-20), insulate the unit, keep battery warm or remove it. |
| **Tropical/North** (Darwin, Cairns) | Salt corrosion, fungal growth, high humidity condensation. | Apply dielectric grease to terminals, use desiccant packs, run monthly to dry internals. |

> **Safety First Box:** Before touching any component, ensure the generator is cool and off. Wear nitrile gloves and safety goggles when handling oil or coolant. Never service a generator in an enclosed space without ventilation due to carbon monoxide risks from residual fumes. Dispose of used oil and stabiliser at your local council waste depot—never pour it down the drain.

**Sizing Your Storage:** If you're investing in a case, match it to your unit. A 2 kW portable needs roughly 0.5 m³ of internal volume; a 10 kW standby unit requires a minimum 3 m³ enclosure with airflow channels.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does fuel stabiliser actually last in a stored diesel generator?**  
Modern diesel-specific stabilisers protect against oxidation, water emulsification, and microbial growth for up to 12 months when dosed correctly. In Australia’s humid or tropical regions, I’d refresh it at the six-month mark to prevent phase separation and “diesel bug” contamination.

**Can I skip the oil filter change during seasonal storage?**  
Absolutely not. Old oil holds acids, soot, and microscopic wear metals that accelerate internal corrosion while the engine sits. A fresh spin-on filter paired with new 5W-40 diesel oil ensures clean lubrication for your first hot start months later.

**Is a generator case really necessary in Australia’s variable climate?**  
Yes, even under a carport. Sudden downpours, salt spray, and UV degradation will eat through wiring insulation and corrode control panels over time. A ventilated, insulated case like the one listed blocks moisture while allowing safe heat dissipation.

**Why does my generator battery die despite being on a smart charger?**  
Smart chargers maintain voltage but won’t revive a sulfated or age-weary 12 V lead-acid battery. If it’s over four years old and refuses to hold a load during cranking, replace it. Modern lithium-compatible smart chargers handle AGM and flooded types well—just verify cold-cranking amp (CCA) compatibility first.
