### Step 1: Safety First – Power Down and Prep

In Australia, our homes run on **240 V/50 Hz**. This isn't just a number; it's the standard your fan must be rated for, and it’s exactly why we take safety seriously. Before anything else, go to your switchboard and isolate the circuit where you’ll be working. Flip the breaker to OFF, then verify it’s dead using a voltage tester or multimeter on the ceiling rose. Never trust the switch alone—always test twice.

**Compliance Checklist (AS/NZS 3816 & Local Codes):**
- ✅ Fan is rated for indoor use and matches your ceiling height (minimum 2.1m clearance).
- ✅ Mounting hardware is rated for dynamic load (fans move; static boxes aren’t enough).
- ✅ All wiring connections are secured with approved wire nuts or Wago connectors.
- ✅ Earth/ground wire is correctly terminated to the fan canopy and bracket.
- ✅ Local council rules allow DIY electrical work in your state (note: NSW, VIC, QLD, and WA have strict limits; if unsure, hire a licensed electrician for the final connection).

**⚠️ Sarah’s “Do Not” List:**
- Don’t use a damaged or wobbly ladder.
- Don’t force wires into tight junction boxes; it causes heat buildup.
- Don’t skip the mounting brace—even lightweight fans vibrate over time.
- Don’t mix aluminium and copper wiring without anti-oxidant paste.

### Step 2: Mark, Brace, and Secure Your Mounting Point

Ceiling fans don’t hang from plasterboard—they hang from timber or steel joists. Lay out your fan’s mounting template on the ceiling, then use your stud finder to locate a solid joist directly beneath it. If the joist aligns perfectly, great! If not, install a retrofit ceiling brace (like a Hilti or Gib fix) between two joists before attaching the bracket. Slide your heavy-duty mounting plate over the box, align it with your template holes, and secure it with 75mm timber screws. Give it a firm tug; if it doesn’t budge, you’re ready for wiring.

### Step 3: Connect the Wiring (The Scary Part, Made Simple)

Take a breath—you’ve got this. Most Australian ceiling roses have three wires: live (red/brown), neutral (black/blue), and earth (green/yellow). Your fan will match these colours. Strip about 1

...mm of insulation from each wire end using your wire strippers. Connect live to live, neutral to neutral, and earth to earth—never cross them! Secure each connection with an AS/NZS 3123-rated lever-nut or twist connector, then neatly tuck the wires back into the ceiling rose or junction box. Ensure absolutely no bare copper is exposed outside the terminals.

### Step 4: Mount, Balance & Power Up
Hang your fan’s canopy over the mounting bracket, secure it with the provided hardware, and attach the blades according to the manufacturer’s torque specs. Before restoring power, double-check that all screws are tight, blades are level, and no tools or debris remain in the housing. Flip your circuit breaker back on, test the pull-chain or remote, and if you notice a slight wobble after the first run, use a balancing kit on the blade hooks. A quiet, steady spin means you’re done.

### FAQs
**Can I legally install a ceiling fan myself in Australia?**  
Yes, provided you’re only reconnecting existing switched-live circuits and not altering the main wiring or adding new power points. Any new circuit work must be completed by a licensed electrician to comply with AS/NZS 3000.

**How do I pick the right fan size?**  
Match blade span to room area: 1000–1200mm for up to 9m², 1200–1400mm for 9–14m², and 1400mm+ for larger rooms. Maintain at least 2.1m of clearance between the blades and floor.

**Why is my fan wobbling or humming?**  
Wobbling usually stems from unbalanced blades, loose mounting hardware, or a misaligned downrod. Tighten all brackets, check blade alignment, and use a balancing kit if needed. Persistent humming often indicates a failing capacitor—call an electrician.

**Do I need smart features or a remote?**  
Not mandatory, but highly practical. Look for fans with built-in RF remotes or Wi-Fi/Tuya compatibility that integrate with your existing home automation setup.

**When should I stop DIY and call a pro?**  
If you’re upgrading circuits, dealing with steel joists without proper braces, working in homes with outdated wiring, or simply feel uncertain at any stage. Compliance and safety always outweigh pride.
